## Context

The founder: **one-tap from notifications is the pitch.** In the Robinhood style: pull the notification down without opening the app, see the proposal, order or mail, and act on it there. That collides with ADR 0112, which says approving from a notification lands on the seal and never on the tap.

The one-tap research (lane research, 2026-09-19: iOS, Android, lock screen, UX, safety, and two adversaries, both "wounded") established:

- **Expo alone cannot act in the shade.** iOS never runs JS for a background notification response (`expo-notifications` 0.32.16, `ExpoHandlingDelegate.kt:140-144`, re-read locally). On Android the Expo push relay turns any push with a title and body into an FCM notification message. The system tray draws it with no custom buttons, and the data-only path "can not be triggered by expo push service" (`ExpoHandlingDelegate.kt:122-128`; Expo docs, modified 2026-08-07). Every in-shade act needs native Swift (notification extensions) and Kotlin (our own builder).
- **In-shade approval is real only on an unlocked phone.** A locked iPhone forwards the action to the app. An Android key that needs recent strong authentication fails after a weak face unlock, after Smart Lock, or more than 30 s after unlock.
- **Push reaches no phone today.** Production `mobile_devices` had 0 rows on 2026-09-19 (lane measurement, not re-queried here). The app has never run on a device (OD-109).
- **Live defects on main that any one-tap path would inherit** (re-read at `origin/main` `e066712bc`):

## Options considered

1. **Approve on the tap, from the lock screen.** **Rejected:** anyone holding the phone could spend. It breaks ADR 0112's line that a notification lands on the seal, and a locked phone cannot do it anyway.
2. **Phase 1 only: one tap to the seal sheet, forever.** It is safe and needs no native code. **Not chosen as the end:** it stops short of the pitch.
3. **Staged: Phase 1 now, Phase 2 in the shade under a ceiling, dark by default.** *Chosen.*
4. **Approve from an email link.** **Rejected:** mail scanners pre-fetch links, and a link is neither a hold nor an identity (ADR 0174, decision 5).
5. **Keep auto-send behind its flags.** **Rejected:** "every send needs a tap". A letter cannot be recalled.
6. **Approve the order now and the letter later.** **Rejected:** two decisions, and the second is easy to skip. The order tap would publish a letter nobody read (`procurement.service.ts:3527` drafts it after approval today).
7. **Hide mobile Approve until a mobile seal exists.** Honest, but **rejected** for porting the web's hold and challenge now, which is the web's own bar.

## Decision

1. **Phase 1: the tap opens straight onto the seal sheet.** No native code. The tap is not the intent. Identity comes from the OS unlock and Face ID, and intent from the gesture on the sheet (ADR 0176). Pulled down on an unlocked phone, the notification shows the proposal. (On iOS in Phase 1, for a phone set to "When Unlocked" that has a passcode. Elsewhere, and on Android, the proposal is one tap away until Phase 2; see decision 3, batches 8 and 9.) "Not now" clears the card and keeps the inbox row.
2. **Phase 2: approve inside the notification shade with a hold.** It is dark by default and on only when the house turns it on. It applies only within the house's limits below, which start at 0 (off, so no default spends: ADR 0116), and only on an unlocked phone. It needs native Swift and Kotlin, and it is built only after the device spikes pass. Over either limit, or on a locked phone, the same notification falls back to Phase 1. Its first act is the order approval drawn in mock 3f. *Lane research recommendation, proposed (`onetap/safety.md`, lane scratchpad, not in the repo):* payments, bank details, grants and configuration stay on the seal sheet. It needs the ADR 0112 amendment recorded in ADR 0176.
   **Founder limits (2026-09-19, batch 5).** The ceiling is two limits, and both start at 0: a **per-order ceiling** and a **per-house daily cap** on in-shade approvals. An enrolled phone is **unenrolled automatically** on a password reset or an in-app password change (ADR 0174 D8), when the member is removed from the house, and when the member's role changes.
3. **The lock-screen rule.** Locked, a notification shows the event, the house, the order number and a count. Unlocked, it adds the vendor, the amounts and the draft. Email bodies and bank details never leave the app. **A house cannot override this.**
   **Founder decisions (2026-09-19, batches 7 and 8): the payload is rich on iOS and lock-safe on Android until Phase 2.** On iOS the payload carries the vendor, the amounts, the draft and the vendor-mail gist, so an unlocked pull-down shows the proposal in Phase 1 without native code, and the OS redacts the lock screen. On Android it does not: the #403 adversary read the Android source and found that Android resets a channel's app-set `lockscreenVisibility` (`PreferencesHelper.createNotificationChannel`, "Reset fields that apps aren't allowed to set"), and its default is to show all notification content on a locked phone. So a rich Android push would break the rule above.
   - **Android (batch 8):** the push carries only the locked fields: the event, the house, the order number and a count. The proposal is seen after one tap into the app. A rich Android pull-down waits for Phase 2 native code, which can fetch the details after unlock or build a redacted public version from a data-only message.
   - **iOS (batch 7; superseded by batch 9 below):** the rich payload with a lock-safe placeholder, relying on "Show Previews: When Unlocked". That is the default on Face ID devices; it is not verified for every Touch ID model, where "Always" may be the default.
   - **iOS, per phone (batch 9):** each iPhone reports its preview setting (`getPermissionsAsync().ios.allowsPreviews`) and whether it has a passcode, and the server picks the payload for that phone. A phone that would show previews while locked, because its previews are set to "Always" or it has no passcode, gets only the locked fields: the event, the house, the order number and a count. Phones set to "When Unlocked" keep the rich pull-down. This needs no native code, and the lock-screen rule then holds on every iPhone as of its last report, whatever its default. A setting changed after that report is exposed until the next one; see the build notes. **Founder decision (2026-09-19, batch 10):** a phone that has not reported gets the lock-safe payload (fail closed), and so does a phone whose last report is **more than 7 days old**. Reports refresh every time the app comes to the foreground.
   - **Build notes (from the #406 and #407 gates; they implement batches 9 and 10 and are not new decisions):**
     - Send the rich payload only when `allowsPreviews` is `WHEN_AUTHENTICATED` and `expo-local-authentication` `getEnrolledLevelAsync()` is not `SecurityLevel.NONE`. `isEnrolledAsync()` reports biometrics only, so it is the wrong check. `NEVER`, `ALWAYS` and a missing value all get lock-safe fields.
     - Re-report on every app foreground, and store the report's own `reported_at`. Do not reuse the device's `last_seen_at`: `expo-push.service.ts` `registerDevice` refreshes it on every registration.
     - Choose the payload per token. `sendToTokens` sends one payload to many phones, so split each send by payload class.
     - Accept a report only for tokens owned by the caller.
     - Stale means `reported_at` is more than 7 days old (batch 10). The server stamps `reported_at` when it receives the report and never takes a client clock.
     - `registerDevice` clears the report fields when it creates a row or when a token changes owner, so the new row counts as unreported until the phone reports.
     - A paired Apple Watch mirrors notifications under its own privacy setting, and this rule does not cover it.
   Costs the founder accepted: on iOS, the vendor names, amounts, draft and vendor-mail gist transit Expo and APNs. Email bodies and bank details are never in any payload. Rejected alternatives: (a) lock-safe on both platforms, which costs iOS its Phase 1 pull-down proposal; (b) a data-only Android push in Phase 1, which needs a background handler that some phones kill; (c) accepting the Android lock-screen exposure, which breaks the rule. Notifications carry the real Mudavym logo (`apps/web/public/logo.png`), as in the approved mockup.
4. **Vendor-mail push.** It shows the sender, the subject and a one-line AI gist, on an unlocked phone only. Locked, it shows only that a vendor wrote. (On Android in Phase 1, and on an iPhone that gets the lock-safe payload, the push says only that a vendor wrote, locked or not; see decision 3, batches 8 and 9.)
5. **Every vendor auto-send is retired.** Every send needs a person's tap. The four paths above are removed, not flagged off. **This reverses OD-37** (`OPEN-DECISIONS.md:112`), which was struck on 2026-08-26 with auto-send accepted behind a full-autonomy switch and a 2-minute undo. ADR 0118 D2 takes the house-mailbox undo window from that path's `AUTO_SEND_UNDO_MS` (`inbound-responder.service.ts:41`). The 2-minute house window stands; when the constant goes with the auto-send path, D2's window must be declared where the house letter is queued.
6. **One approval covers the order and its letter (`approve_and_send`).** The push waits until the letter is drafted. The seal approves the order and sends that exact letter: the letter's content and recipients are bound into the seal, so an edit is a new version and needs a new seal.
7. **`approveOrder` always needs owner, manager or a grantee** (ADR 0112 F12's authority rule), even when no ADR 0116 rule fires.
8. **The web's hold and challenge is ported to mobile approve now.** On the phone the gesture is the swipe (ADR 0176).
9. **Every vendor send is sealed, and the seal binds what is sent** (founder, 2026-09-19, batch 5). `approve-draft`, `manual-reply`, `confirm-deal` and `POST /conversations/:id/approve` each take the seal. The seal binds the exact recipients, CC and text, so a sealed send sends only server-held content and any edit is a new version that needs a new seal. "Counts match" gets the seal too. *Lane research recommendation, proposed:* this is Phase 0, every defect in the table is a build prerequisite for Phase 1, and each goes on its own branch.
10. **Every vendor send needs owner, manager or a grantee** (founder, 2026-09-19, batch 5), the same rule as `approveOrder` in decision 7. This covers the four routes in decision 9. `/conversations/:id/approve` today admits owner and manager only (`conversations.controller.ts:428`), so a grantee is added there.

## Consequences

- **The honest pitch is "never more than one prompt", not "never opens the app".** Phase 1 is roughly tap, Face ID, swipe.
- **Phase 2's proof is narrower than it sounds.** It proves that this enrolled phone, unlocked, approved these figures. It does not prove whose face it was at that instant. The two limits bound that residual, and unenrollment on reset, removal or role change bounds how long it lasts.
- **Native code brings a release train.** Extensions, a Kotlin module, EAS signing for three iOS bundle ids, and an app-store build for every new act. Research recommends a server-driven card with categories named by rung rather than by kind, so copy ships without a binary.
- **Prerequisites the research names:** an Expo access token on the sender (`expo-push.service.ts` sends none), the push pipe itself, and preferences that can be saved.
- **Collision flagged, not resolved:** ADR 0160 (on `feat/mudavym-finish`, not main) offers an auto-approve ceremony for hold-to-order. Under decision 5, auto-approve can never send a vendor letter.
- **Revisit when** a spike shows in-shade signing is unreliable on a common device, or a house asks to go above its limits.

## Open

Not decided here, and not added to `OPEN-DECISIONS.md` (see ADR 0173's register note).

- **Time-sensitive events.** Which events break through Focus.
- **Android key semantics.** The strong-auth window, and what happens after face unlock or Smart Lock.
- **Native-code ownership.** Who owns the EAS, Apple Developer and Firebase accounts, and who maintains the Swift and Kotlin.
- **The server send-window length.** How long an approved letter is held so Undo can stop it (the mock shows 5 s; the existing window is 2 minutes).
- **Device install-exclusivity.** Does a second person signing in make an install permanently shared, with no in-shade approval?
- **The bundle-id rename** from `ai.wineops.mobile` (`apps/mobile/app.json:18,29`). It must come before any key group exists.
- **Also owed:** a written acceptance of the Phase 2 residual before any house turns it on, and which acts join the order approval in the shade.

## Amendment 2026-09-21 — "Staff ask, manager sends": decisions 9 and 10, built

**Founder's answer (2026-09-21), as recorded in the lane brief, verbatim:** *"the draft-send seal is REQUIRED on every vendor send now - delete the REQUIRE_DRAFT_SEND_SEAL grace (OD-109: the phone app has 0 installs; re-state that as measured-by-record, you cannot query production). An owner, a manager, or a person an owner has granted sends with one hold. When a staff member holds, the letter becomes a REQUEST: save the staffer's exact edited text as the version, record who asked, mark the draft waiting for a manager, notify managers and owners (web bell now); a manager releases it with one hold over that exact text; an edit is a new version needing a new seal; the staffer sees who sent it. Close the three unsealed doors in the same pass: manual-reply, confirm-deal, and /conversations/:id/approve (ADR 0175 D9/D10) with an actor recorded"*, and for grants: *"the ADR 0112 F12 grant row (grantor, grantee, scope, limit, expiry, revoked_at), only an owner issues, any owner revokes, every grant/revocation told to all owners, 'granted by' shown where used."* He chose it after the lane research (judge: option 1, "Ask a manager, owner grants"; `q921/seal-judge.md`, lane scratchpad, not in the repo). The hierarchy is the one he set on 2026-09-05 (ADR 0112 F12 ruling 2) and 2026-09-19 (decision 10 above); this amendment builds it and adds the request state.

**Why the grace could go, by record (not measured).** `REQUIRE_DRAFT_SEND_SEAL` existed for native installs predating the seal. OD-109 records that the app has never been run on a device, and this ADR's context records 0 production `mobile_devices` rows on 2026-09-19. This lane may not read production, so neither was re-measured; the repo has no `eas.json`, no `expo-updates` and no `ios/`/`android/` directory, so no installed build could exist to strand. Every web and phone client in this lane already sends a seal.

**What is built (lane E, 2026-09-21):**

1. **The seal is required, with no flag.** `legacyDraftSendMayGoUnsealed` and the env var are deleted; `approveDraft` takes an actor and always redeems, so an absent challenge is refused as "absent" like every other sealed act.
2. **One authority rule for every vendor send.** `decideVendorSend` (`organizations/vendor-send-authority.ts`, pure) and `VendorSendAuthorityService` (strict reads: a role or grant that cannot be read is a 500, never "ask" and never "send") gate the mint and the send of: approve-draft / send-drafted-reply, manual-reply, confirm-deal, `POST /conversations/:id/approve` (its `@Roles` is replaced, because a grantee is a row, not a token role), and the house composer `POST /communications/letters` (below).
3. **A staff hold is a request.** `POST orders/:id/draft-send-request` saves the person's exact words as the draft's `content`, and records `send_requested_by/_at/_sha256/_cc` (migration 20260926140100). The draft stays `PENDING_APPROVAL`; "waiting for a manager" is that plus a request. The request is **current** only while the draft's words still hash to the requested version, so any later edit or regenerate reads as "no longer their version" without every writer having to clear a flag. With no draft waiting, the staffer's own reply becomes the waiting draft, threaded to the vendor's last message. Owners and managers are told on the web bell (priority `low`, so the notification funnel does not also push it to phones: a push would carry a vendor's and a person's name to a locked screen, which decision 3 forbids until its per-phone payload is built).
4. **The release** is the ordinary sealed send over the stored words and the requester's copies; an edit is a new version under the releaser's own new seal. Every sent row records `sent_by_user_id` (and `sent_under_grant_id` for a grantee); the requester is told who sent it and whether it went as they wrote it. Thread readouts carry `sentByName` and `requestedByName`.
5. **The three doors.** `manual-reply` and `confirm-deal` have their own mint routes and acts (`send_manual_reply`, `confirm_deal`); confirm-deal's seal is over the terms and the address (its letter is composed on the server from them) and it now writes `approved_by`. `/conversations/:id/approve` is sealed over the message it would release (subject kind `procurement_conversation`, act `approve_conversation`). No live web client calls it today (`ConversationApprovalNotification.tsx` has no importer and sends no token). Its actor is recorded only on the redeemed seal (`mcp_seal_challenges.actor_user_id`), not on the row: the route's existing update writes five columns — `manager_approval_status`, `manager_approved_message`, `approval_channel`, `resumed_at`, `time_to_approval_seconds` — that no migration declares (baseline included), so whether that write can succeed in production at all was not checked. Observed, not fixed.
6. **The composer is a fourth door.** Decision 9 lists four routes and does not name `POST /communications/letters`. The lane judge found it is a vendor send (every recipient it accepts is in the vendor book), and the lane brief said to gate it in that case; it is sealed (subject kind `house_letter`, keyed on the vendor) and gated. It has **no request path**: a staff member sees why before the hold, not after. **[Superseded 2026-09-21 by answer 3 of the second amendment below: a staff member asks, and a manager releases the saved letter with one hold.]** Its house-mailbox ceremony stays a click with an undo window (ADR 0118 D2), now minting the seal on the click.
7. **Grants** (`authority_grants`, migration 20260926140000): grantor, grantee, scope (`vendor_send` only), limit + currency, expiry, revoked-at/by; grantor ≠ grantee enforced by a CHECK. `GET/POST /authority/grants`, `POST /authority/grants/:id/revoke`: a grant counts only while its grantee still holds a role in the house (issue refuses a non-member, and the gate refuses one too — a last-call fix: before it, a live grant held by someone who had left was decided "send"); only an owner issues, any owner revokes, the owner must answer limit and expiry explicitly (a missing key is refused; no default stands in), and every issue and revocation is told to every owner and the grantee. "Granted by" is shown on every panel that holds a letter and on `/team`'s new "Who may send to vendors" section.
8. **Readout.** `GET orders/:id/draft` returns `sendOrAsk` beside the draft (`mayApprove`'s shape: `readable`, `maySend`, `mode`, `basis`, `grant`, `sentence`); the composer's sender readout carries the same field. `/orders` (DraftRail, DraftEmailApprovalPanel, the thread drawer's reply box and deal confirmation), `/communications` (DraftedReplyPanel, the composer) and the phone draft screen show "hold to send" or "hold to ask a manager" before the hold.

**Forks this build had to take a side on, recorded here for the founder rather than decided (no new OD rows, per the lane rule):**

- **A grant with no money limit covers letters only.** `decideVendorSend` refuses a limitless grant for an act that carries money (confirm-deal), reading ADR 0116's "a default is not an answer" as "an absent limit is not unlimited". The alternative is "no limit means unlimited".
- **A grant dies with its grantor's ownership.** A grant from someone who is no longer an owner here does not count (authority cannot outlive its source). The alternative is that a grant stands until revoked whatever happens to the grantor. **[Answered 2026-09-21, answer 1 of the second amendment below: it stops at once and waits for a current owner.]**
- **Staff cannot request a deal confirmation or a composer letter.** Only drafted letters and thread replies have a request path; confirm-deal and the composer refuse staff before the hold with who can act instead. **[Answered 2026-09-21, answer 3: staff may ask for both.]**
- **Issuing a grant is a hold, not a server seal.** F12 treats a grant as a security change; the server does not yet seal it, and F12's single `security_events` ledger does not exist, so the grant row and the owners' notices are the record. **[Answered 2026-09-21, answer 4: sealed, and the ledger is built.]**
- **Who may read the register of grants.** Built: owners see every grant; everyone else, managers included, sees only the grants that name them (so a manager's `/team` says "No grant names you", not "Nobody has been named"). The alternative is that managers — who release staff requests — also read the whole register. **[Answered 2026-09-21, answer 2: managers read it; an owner can mark a grant owner-only.]**
- **The composer's undo ceremony** mints its seal on the click. Whether a house-mailbox letter should become a hold now that every vendor send is sealed is the founder's call. **[Answered 2026-09-21, answer 7: it stays a click with the 2-minute undo.]**

**Not built here:** the swipe gesture on the phone (ADR 0176) — the phone keeps the hold; push delivery of these notices (decision 3's payload rules first); `approveOrder`'s decision 7 (`ADR-0175-APPROVE-ORDER-ALWAYS-NEEDS-AUTHORITY` stays open); the auto-send retirement of decision 5.

## Second amendment 2026-09-21 — the founder's answers on the forks, built

**Source.** The founder answered the first amendment's forks the same day. His answers reach this record through the lane brief (the orchestrator's relay, round 2); only the words in quotation marks below are his verbatim, the rest is the relay's wording, kept close. Answer 8-12 of the same batch are recorded in [[0192-received-is-the-shelf-count-from-the-ledger]] and [[0083-a-page-may-not-claim-a-write-it-never-makes]].

1. **A grant whose issuing owner is demoted or removed stops at once, and the row stays** as "awaiting an owner's re-approval"; only a current owner's approval re-activates it (under the seal), or an owner deletes it; nothing re-activates by itself — *"no owner grant, no activation, or no going back once grant author gone"*. **Built:** a grant now rests on an owner (`vouched_by_user_id`: the grantor at issue, the re-approving owner after a re-approval) and carries a latch (`suspended_at`). Triggers on `user_restaurant_access` and on the legacy `users` row — the two places `readRestaurantRole` reads a role from — latch every live grant resting on a person the moment that person stops being an owner of the house, by any door (role change, removal, leaving, a deleted account), write the event to the ledger and put a notice on every owner's bell and the grantee's (`20260926140600`). Only `authority_grant_reapprove`, which refuses anyone who is not an owner now, clears the latch; an owner may instead delete the waiting grant. The gate refuses a latched grant whatever its owner is today, and a grant resting on a non-owner even if a trigger was ever bypassed. `/team` lists waiting grants under "Waiting for an owner" with hold-to-re-approve and hold-to-delete for owners.

Every grant change — issue, revoke, re-approve, delete, owner-only, the latch — is one database function that changes the grant and appends its event in one transaction; `scripts/check_grant_writes_are_ledgered.py` (blocking in CI) fails when app code writes either table any other way. A send made under a grant is written as `grant_relied_on` after the seal and before anything leaves; if the ledger cannot be written, nothing is sent (F12: "grant checks write to one tamper-evident chain").

## Third amendment 2026-09-21 — a staff request can be declined or withdrawn, and an undone release waits again

**Source.** The founder, 2026-09-21, on the gap the round-2 last call stated (verbatim): *"Decline/withdraw; undo re-waits"*.

**Built (lane E round 3):**

1. **Decline.** An owner or a manager declines a waiting letter request with a reason (1 to 500 characters): `POST /communications/letters/requests/:id/decline`. The role is read strictly from the signed token's person (`isOwnerOrManager` over the authority's standing read), so a failed read refuses. A staff member or a grantee may not decline. The close is conditional on `waiting`: a request released a moment earlier is not declined after the fact (409). The person who asked is told on the bell who declined it and why, and that nothing was sent.
2. **Withdraw.** Only the person who asked withdraws their own waiting request: `POST /communications/letters/requests/:id/withdraw`. A manager declines instead. The owners and managers are told on the bell.
3. **On the record.** `20260926141100` adds `closed_how` (`declined` | `withdrawn` | `deal_dismissed`), `closed_by` (on `public.users(user_id)`) and `closed_at`, and a CHECK that a closed row says how and when. The one earlier closer (`closeWaitingDeal`) now writes `deal_dismissed` with the time. A dismissal names no person.
4. **Undo re-waits.** A released letter pulled back inside the composer's 2-minute undo window puts its request back to `waiting`. The release fields are cleared, and `undone_count`, `last_undone_at` and `last_undone_by` record the pull-back. The person who asked is told who pulled it back and that it was not sent.
   - The request is found by the cancelled letter's id and changed only *after* the cancel has landed. That order matters: a request re-opened while its letter could still leave could be released twice. **[Last call, 2026-09-21: found only through the release's best-effort link, so a link that did not land left the request `released`, silently, after its letter was pulled back. The letter now carries its request's id (`email_headers.request_id`, stamped at release), and the pull-back finds the request by that id: still `released`, and linked to this letter or never linked. A letter queued before the stamp is still found by its own id. A stamped request that no longer reads as released by this letter is said in the cancel's answer and logged, never a quiet "pulled back".]**
   - The cancel itself is now conditional on the letter still being queued and on its house. It used to answer "pulled back" even when the dispatcher had taken the letter between the read and the write.
   - The panel that releases a letter now offers "Pull it back" while the window is open.
5. **What the panel shows.** `/communications`' "Letters waiting for a manager" offers Decline (with the reason box) to an owner or a manager, and "Withdraw my request" to the person who asked. A request that was pulled back says it is waiting again.

**Not built, stated.**
- The service methods take a deal request's kind as well, but no route declines or withdraws a waiting *deal* request. The founder's answer names letter requests; a waiting deal request still leaves `waiting` only when its deal is confirmed (released) or dismissed (closed).
- If the re-wait fails after a pull-back, the letter stays pulled back and the answer says the request could not be put back. The request is not retried.
- If the requester or the managers cannot be told, that is logged. It does not refuse the close.
- **[Last call, 2026-09-21]** A released letter that the dispatcher then fails to send (`FAILED`) leaves its request `released`; only a pull-back puts a request back to waiting. Whether a failed send should re-wait the request too is not decided here. **[E4, 2026-09-22: decided, "Back to waiting", and built (fourth amendment).]**

**Evidence.** `house-letters-sealed.spec.ts`: decline with a reason; owner yes, staff and grantee no; a role that cannot be read refused; blank reason refused; withdraw by the asker only; no close after a release, or in a lost race; re-wait after undo; no request touched by an unrelated letter; a failed re-wait said; a dispatcher-taken letter not reported pulled back; the viewer flag. Also `LetterRequests.test.tsx`: decline, withdraw, re-waiting line, pull-back and a failed pull-back. PGlite probe `E3-migrations.mjs`. CLAIMS row `ADR-0175-REQUEST-DECLINE-WITHDRAW-REWAIT`.

## Fourth amendment 2026-09-22 — a released letter that fails to send puts its request back to waiting

**Source.** The founder's answer to the round-3 lane report's question 4, relayed in the round-6u lane brief and dated there 2026-09-22. His pick, verbatim: *"Back to waiting (Recommended)"*. As the brief states it: a released staff letter the dispatcher fails to send returns its request to waiting in the manager's queue, with the failure reason shown to the manager and the staffer; `released` never stands on an unsent letter.

**Built (lane E round 4):**

1. **The dispatcher re-waits.** When `dispatchDue` records a letter as failed, it calls `VendorSendRequestsService.rewaitAfterFailedSend`. That goes through the ONE re-wait the pull-back uses (`rewaitReleased`): found by the request id the letter carries or by the letter's own id, and written only while the request is still released by THIS letter. It clears the release and writes, in the same statement, `send_failed_count`, `last_send_failed_at`, `last_send_failure` (the dispatcher's words, trimmed to 300 characters) and `last_send_failed_by` (the manager who released it) (20260926141400, with a CHECK that a failure is counted, dated and said together).
2. **Both people are told why.** The person who asked: "<manager> released your letter to <vendor>, but it could not be sent: <reason> Your request is waiting for an owner or a manager again." The manager who released it: "The letter you released for <asker> to <vendor> could not be sent: <reason> The request is back in the managers' queue, waiting." The panel shows the reason on the waiting request (`letter-request-send-failed`).
3. **A sent letter is never called failed.** Once Gmail accepted the letter, nothing after it may mark it failed or re-wait its request (that would let a manager release the same letter twice): a failure to record SENT is logged, and the letter counts as sent. Before, a throw after the send marked a sent letter `HOUSE_FAILED`.
4. **`released` does not stand on an unsent letter.** A re-wait that could not land (the request could not be written) is retried at the start of the next run: released house-letter requests whose linked letter reads failed go back to waiting, with the reason the letter recorded.
5. **Each dispatcher failure in its own words.** A mailbox with no send grant used to fail as "the queued letter has no recipient recorded"; that sentence is now shown to people, so each cause is worded as itself.

**Not built, stated.**
- **A letter stuck in `SENDING`** (the dispatcher claimed it and then stopped) keeps its request `released`; whether it left is not known from the row, so it is not re-waited.
- **The retry looks at the newest 100 released requests** each run, and only at those linked to their letter; a request whose release link never landed is re-waited on the failing run only (found by the letter's stamped request id).
- **A request re-waited after a failed send is not released again automatically**; a manager releases it, as after a pull-back.
- **Unverified here:** no browser check and no production read.

**Evidence.** `house-letters-sealed.spec.ts` (the failed send re-waits, on the record, both told; Gmail's own refusal kept as the reason; a sent letter whose record failed is neither failed nor re-waited; a letter that released no request touches none; a request another letter carries is untouched; a re-wait that did not land is retried next run), `LetterRequests.test.tsx`, PGlite `E4-migrations.mjs`. CLAIMS row `ADR-0175-FAILED-SEND-REWAITS`.
