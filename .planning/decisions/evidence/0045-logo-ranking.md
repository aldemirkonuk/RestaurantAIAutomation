# The 17-candidate ranking — ordered under ADR 0045

Founder's order, 2026-08-30: *"rank all of them just because i want to for our
goal"* — the mark is already chosen (the Rivet M, outside this set); this ranking
is the record of what the search produced and where each candidate actually
stood. Method: every sheet rendered from the published canvas's own sources
(headless Chrome, both grounds) and **looked at**; the lineage wave additionally
carries session -29's true-16px rasterisation data (`CANDIDATES.md`), used as
measured evidence. Criteria are OD-111's five: **C1** 16–24px survival ·
**C2** no asymmetric cutouts · **C3** uniform stroke · **C4** reads M first ·
**C5** no lookalike/costume liability. Secondary lens ("our goal"): **seal
duty** — can the mark be the object a hold-to-approve completes into — and
signal capability (chroma as anomaly).

## The ranking

| # | Candidate (wave) | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Full Stop** (lineage) | The measured strongest: "M." reads instantly at a true 16px on both grounds, every criterion satisfied by construction, C5 clear (no dominant owner of "M."; nearest neighbours are fashion monograms, a different category). |
| 2 | **The Double Rule M** (lineage) | All five clean by construction; the accountant's mark under a total is the house story in one object. Only soft spot: at 16px the two rules are *just* separable and the lower one greys toward the charcoal ground. |

| 4 | **The Hallmark** (instrument F) | Fraunces M in a notched assay die, stop shed below 96px — M-first at every size, natural stamp behaviour. Its own sheet is honest that letter-in-a-frame is the most conventional construction here; it lives on the die shape. |
| 5 | **Folding Rule** (instrument C) | The best pure-M silhouette of the twelve wave marks — and the Rivet M's conceptual cousin (jointed strokes, riveted hinges). Falls to 5 because everything distinctive is shed below 96px, leaving a plain zigzag that drifts mountain-logo/heartbeat (C5). |

## The Rivet M against the same bar (the winner, screened, not exempted)

- **C1:** strokes at 13u with open-wedge counters; the mono cut swaps the filled
  rivet for a washer ring precisely so 16px doesn't muddy. Pass.
- **C2/C3:** bilaterally symmetric; one stroke width, rivet as element not cutout.
  Pass by construction.
- **C4:** the M reads first; the rivet reads second as punctuation. Pass.
- **C5, screened honestly:** no dominant owner found for "angular M with a vertex
  dot". Genre adjacencies worth knowing: in mono at small sizes the vertex dot
  can read as a *location pin between peaks* (outdoor/map genre), and
  round-capped zigzag Ms are common in generic monogram space. Neither is a
  named-mark collision of the VS-Code kind that killed the bowtie. **No blocker
  found; the adjacency note stands as the thing to re-check when the mark meets
  the seal-stamp animation.**

## What the ranking says about "our goal"

The top of the table is unanimous on one thing the chosen mark does *not* give
us: **"M." — the full stop — is the search's strongest small-size device**, and
it already lives in the product (sketch 081's double-rule/full-stop idiom, the
retired interim glyph). The Rivet M wins the identity; the full stop remains the
best fallback vocabulary for contexts the rivet cannot reach. And the strongest
*seal-duty* candidates (Housemark, Bezel, Punch) all argue the same design fact:
the approval landing wants a **contained** object — so when the Rivet M becomes
the hold-to-approve stamp, expect it to need a ring or die around it, exactly as
sketch 083 specified for the house seal.
