## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid — Multi-Agent Orchestration + Structured Data Extraction

**Description:**
This system is an AI-powered email intelligence pipeline embedded in a fine-dining restaurant's beverage procurement workflow. It classifies inbound vendor Gmail as OPERATIONAL (order confirmations, invoices, supply issues), PROMO (deals, discounts, limited-time offers), or NOISE (newsletters, surveys, automated receipts), then extracts structured deal data from PROMO emails and surfaces the most actionable offers to restaurant managers and sommeliers via a daily LLM-generated digest and inline dashboard deal cards. A companion ProviderConversationAgent drafts context-aware replies to vendor emails — injecting conversation history, open orders, and active promotions into each reply — which managers approve with one tap before sending. "Good" looks like: no OPERATIONAL email ever falls silent, a Burgundy allocation offer arrives in the morning digest ranked by urgency score on the same day it lands, and every outbound reply reads as if a knowledgeable beverage professional wrote it — not a bot.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. **PROMO classified as NOISE**: A time-limited wine allocation or en primeur offer is silently dropped — the manager never sees it. For fine dining with active cellar programs, allocation windows can close within 24–72 hours; this is a direct revenue loss with no recovery path, and repeat misclassification erodes trust in the entire inbox triage system.
2. **OPERATIONAL classified as NOISE**: A delivery exception, order short-ship, invoice dispute, or account-hold email goes unread until service night. Missing a single OPERATIONAL email can mean running out of a key wine during a busy evening — a Tier 1 service failure.
3. **AI-drafted reply commits to price terms without manager awareness**: ProviderConversationAgent drafts a reply containing language interpretable as price acceptance or order commitment ("we'll take 24 cases at $14.40"). Under UCC / common law, an emailed acceptance with price, quantity, and product constitutes a binding contract. Autonomous replies — especially for `auto_reply_enabled` trusted providers — must never include commitment language before explicit manager review and approval.
4. **Extraction fabricates deal terms not present in the email**: Haiku hallucinates a discount percentage, expiry date, or quantity that does not appear in the vendor email. The manager reads the digest, acts on fabricated pricing, and discovers the real terms differ at order time — creating a procurement dispute and permanently damaging trust in the AI deal-extraction pipeline.
5. **Draft tone signals AI automation to a valued vendor**: A reply that reads as AI-generated (generic structure, no reference to shared history, overly polished formality where the relationship is casual) damages the manager's standing with relationship-dependent distributors. In fine-dining wine procurement, allocation priority is relationship-contingent; a tone misfire with a key rep can cost a restaurant its place in a future allocation list — a consequence that is invisible and effectively irreversible within the season.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Fine dining / upscale restaurant operations — beverage procurement (wine, spirits, specialty ingredients)

**User Population:** Restaurant managers, sommeliers, and beverage directors at upscale establishments. These users manage 5–30 active vendor relationships simultaneously, receive 20–100 vendor emails per day, and make procurement decisions with direct impact on nightly service quality and cellar investment strategy. They are not casual email users — they have deep domain expertise and will immediately recognize classification errors, tone mismatches, or fabricated deal terms.

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** Classified emails determine whether a manager is alerted to a deal or operational issue. Extracted promo data becomes the basis for real procurement decisions. AI-drafted replies go out under the restaurant's name to real vendor relationships after one-tap approval. Errors cascade into: missed deals (direct cost), supply gaps on service nights (guest experience failures), damaged vendor relationships (long-term allocation access loss), and potentially binding purchase commitments (legal/financial risk).

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Classification correctness for limited-availability offers**
- Good (expert accepts): An en primeur Burgundy offer, a flash allocation email from a Rhône importer, or a "48-hour early-access" deal from a spirits rep is classified as PROMO — appears in the morning digest ranked by urgency score on the day it arrives.
- Bad (expert flags): The same email is classified as NOISE or OPERATIONAL. Manager discovers the missed allocation two days later when the window has closed and allocation has been distributed to other accounts.
- Stakes: Critical
- Source: Fine dining procurement practice; Lafleur Wines allocation insider report; Wine-Searcher reporting on Burgundy/DRC allocation dynamics; Yapp Brothers 2024 en primeur offer guidance

**Dimension: Deal term accuracy — price, discount, expiry, quantity**
- Good (expert accepts): The deal card shows "$14.40/bottle (20% off regular $18), 48 cases available, offer expires June 1" — exact figures from the vendor email body, no inference or rounding.
- Bad (expert flags): The card shows "~15% off" when the email said "20% off", or shows an expiry date the email never mentioned, or omits a stated minimum order quantity. Manager approves a reply based on wrong terms, then discovers the discrepancy at invoice time.
- Stakes: Critical
- Source: Procurement professional standards — acting on fabricated pricing creates dispute risk; small-restaurant operations cannot absorb margin errors from bad data extraction

**Dimension: Reply voice — relational warmth calibrated to vendor tier**
- Good (expert accepts): A reply to a well-known distributor rep references a recent tasting or references the current open order in a conversational register that matches how the beverage director actually communicates. The vendor's rep reads it and responds as they would to a person, not a form.
- Bad (expert flags): Generic formal structure ("Dear [Vendor Name], Thank you for reaching out. We would like to inquire further..."), no reference to shared relationship history, robotic pleasantries. Vendor's rep identifies it as AI-generated — relationship credibility damaged, future allocation priority at risk.
- Stakes: High
- Source: GuildSomm beverage director practice; Sommelier Authority wine list development; 2026 B2B trust research on AI-detectable email tone (Tao An; Ry Walker "Trust Collapse in B2B Outbound")

**Dimension: Urgency score calibration — signal reflects genuine procurement priority**
- Good (expert accepts): The deal with the highest urgency score is the one the beverage director would independently choose to act on first — it combines a menu-relevant varietal, a calendar event in the next 14 days needing that wine, a real stock gap, and a genuine expiry window under 72 hours.
- Bad (expert flags): Every third deal scores 8+/10 — manager stops checking urgency scores because they're always inflated. Or the reverse: a 24-hour allocation window scores 3/10 because quantity wasn't explicitly stated, and manager skips it. Either direction produces alert fatigue or missed deals.
- Stakes: High
- Source: Alert-fatigue research in operational AI systems; fine dining procurement practice — beverage directors make allocation calls within hours of receipt for top-tier wines

**Dimension: OPERATIONAL thread completeness — no supply-critical email dropped**
- Good (expert accepts): An invoice dispute, delivery short-ship, account-hold notice, or substitution alert triggers an OPERATIONAL notification and is visible in the provider's conversation history within minutes of arrival. The beverage director can reconstruct the full operational history of any vendor relationship in-app.
- Bad (expert flags): An order short-ship email is classified as NOISE or PROMO. Manager replies to the vendor assuming full delivery is coming, discovers the shortage at receiving on service day — a guest-facing supply failure.
- Stakes: High
- Source: Restaurant operations practice — supply disruptions on service nights are Tier 1 operational failures; no remediation window once service begins

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Allocation window collapse from delayed or missed PROMO classification**: Fine wine allocation emails often expire in 24–72 hours. A classification error, digest-send failure, or false-NOISE routing that delays surfacing a PROMO even by one digest cycle produces unrecoverable opportunity loss. Unlike commodity purchasing, there is no "order it tomorrow from another vendor" fallback for limited-production allocations.

2. **Tone-mismatch reputation damage with allocation-gating distributors**: High-end wine distributors determine allocation priority based on relationship quality and communication consistency. A reply that reads as automated — even once — can shift a restaurant's tier in a distributor's allocation list. This damage is invisible (no distributor explicitly says "your AI email cost you your allocation") and irreversible within a season without deliberate relationship repair.

3. **Contract formation via AI-drafted reply on `auto_reply_enabled` providers**: Draft language naturally includes constructs like "we'd like to proceed with..." or "please confirm our order for..." that, combined with a price and quantity already present in the email thread, create legally ambiguous acceptance signals under UCC Article 2 and common law email exchange doctrine. The `auto_reply_enabled` trusted-vendor path bypasses the approval gate — this is the highest contract-formation risk vector in the pipeline.

4. **Cross-vendor price intelligence false comparison**: When the system surfaces "vs. last purchase: $18 → $14.40," the prior order reference must be the same wine variety, vintage, and format (bottle, 750ml, same appellation). Comparing a 2022 Burgundy BTG pour price to 2024 en primeur futures price as if they're equivalent misleads the manager into a false "savings" signal and a potentially poor procurement decision.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

**Contract formation via email (US common law / UCC — directly relevant):** An AI-drafted reply containing specific price, quantity, and product acceptance language can constitute a binding offer or acceptance under UCC Article 2 (sale of goods) and common law email exchange doctrine. The `auto_reply_enabled` feature must either (a) prohibit drafts containing order quantities, price confirmations, or acceptance language, or (b) add a visible "pending written confirmation" disclaimer to all auto-sent replies. This is the highest-priority regulatory risk in the system.

**Three-tier alcohol beverage system (US — directly relevant):** Restaurants must purchase wine and spirits through licensed distributors, not directly from producers or unlicensed importers. If the AI classifies an email from an unlicensed direct-to-restaurant source as PROMO and creates a deal record, it may facilitate a purchase that violates state ABC licensing requirements. The system does not transact, but should flag vendor emails from sources that do not match a known licensed-distributor list.

**CAN-SPAM Act (US) / CASL (Canada) — low risk, outbound only:** Vendor-to-restaurant emails are transactional B2B communications exempt from CAN-SPAM commercial message requirements. AI-drafted replies are transactional responses, not marketing — no CAN-SPAM exposure from the reply-drafting pipeline under current scope. Risk only materializes if the system were extended to proactively solicit vendors with promotional counter-offers.

**GDPR (relevant if EU-based vendors or EU staff data processed):** Gmail integration processes vendor personal data (names, email addresses, communication history). Legitimate interest under GDPR Article 6(1)(f) provides sufficient legal basis for B2B procurement communication processing. Retention of `procurement_conversations` records should be documented in the restaurant's GDPR record of processing activities if any EU-resident vendor contacts are involved.

**TTB / state ABC boards — indirect:** The procurement facilitation system holds no license. As long as the system only assists human-approved procurement communication and does not autonomously place purchase orders, no ABC board licensing requirement applies to the software layer.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** WineOps BaseAgent + Direct SDK (google-genai + anthropic)

**Version:**
- `google-genai >= 1.0.0` (tested against v1.33.0 — new unified Client API)
- `anthropic >= 0.50.0` (tested against v0.50.x — includes `messages.parse()` structured output)

**Rationale:**
No orchestration framework overhead is needed. The WineOps BaseAgent already handles RabbitMQ subscriptions, idempotency checks, DLQ routing, and the async event loop. Both google-genai and anthropic Python SDKs are sufficiently mature to call directly without a mediating framework. Introducing LangChain or LlamaIndex on top would add abstraction layers without benefit — the email classification and extraction pipelines are linear, not graph-based or retrieval-heavy. The two-SDK approach deliberately splits tasks by model strength: Gemini Flash for high-throughput classification (speed, cost), Claude Haiku for structured extraction and natural-language prose generation (accuracy, compliance with Pydantic schemas). Existing codebase already uses `anthropic` SDK directly in `haiku_enrichment_service.py`; this phase extends the established pattern and adds the google-genai new Client API.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Google ADK | Requires Vertex AI setup; overkill for a two-step classification→extraction pipeline; Gemini Developer API (API key) is sufficient and avoids GCP dependency |
| LangChain | Abstraction overhead without benefit for well-defined linear pipelines; p99 latency degrades under load; adds 15+ transitive dependencies |
| LlamaIndex | RAG/document-retrieval framework — no retrieval needed here; email bodies are passed directly, not indexed |
| CrewAI | Role/crew model maps poorly to a message-driven async pipeline; BaseAgent already handles the equivalent |
| OpenAI Agents SDK | OpenAI vendor lock-in; project already committed to Google + Anthropic providers |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — two providers (Google + Anthropic) for model diversity and cost tiers. Gemini Flash fallback to Haiku classification is explicitly defined (D-01 decision flow). Neither provider is used via an orchestration framework, so switching model versions requires only a settings change, not a framework migration.

### Entry Point Pattern

**EmailIntelAgent — full classification → extraction pipeline:**

```python
import asyncio
from google import genai
from google.genai import types as genai_types
import anthropic
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field
from typing import Optional
from enum import Enum

# ── Output schemas ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
class EmailCategory(str, Enum):
    OPERATIONAL = "OPERATIONAL"
    PROMO = "PROMO"
    NOISE = "NOISE"

class EmailClassification(BaseModel):
    category: EmailCategory
    confidence: float = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0)
    reasoning: str = Field(max_length=200)

class PromoDetails(BaseModel):
    product_name: str
    vendor_name: str
    discount_percent: Optional[float] = None
    original_price_usd: Optional[float] = None
    discounted_price_usd: Optional[float] = None
    validity_date: Optional[str] = None      # ISO date e.g. "2026-06-01"
    wine_variety: Optional[str] = None
    quantity_available: Optional[str] = None

# ── Safety settings for business email (avoid false positives on promo language) ──
BUSINESS_EMAIL_SAFETY = [
    genai_types.SafetySetting(category=cat, threshold="BLOCK_ONLY_HIGH")
    for cat in [
        "HARM_CATEGORY_HARASSMENT",
        "HARM_CATEGORY_HATE_SPEECH",
        "HARM_CATEGORY_SEXUALLY_EXPLICIT",
        "HARM_CATEGORY_DANGEROUS_CONTENT",
    ]
]

CLASSIFICATION_SYSTEM_PROMPT = """You are an email triage agent for a restaurant wine manager.
Classify each email as exactly one of: OPERATIONAL, PROMO, or NOISE.

OPERATIONAL: supplier order confirmations, delivery schedules, invoices, payment terms, account management
PROMO: promotional offers, discounts, special pricing, limited-time deals, new product announcements  
NOISE: newsletters, surveys, automated receipts unrelated to wine orders, spam

Examples:
- "Your order #4521 has shipped" → OPERATIONAL
- "Exclusive 20% off Burgundy this week only" → PROMO
- "Take our customer satisfaction survey" → NOISE

Respond with JSON only. confidence = 0.0–1.0."""

# ── Tier 1: Classify with Gemini Flash ───────────────────────────────────────
async def classify_email(
    gemini_client: genai.Client,
    subject: str,
    sender: str,
    body: str,
) -> EmailClassification:
    body_truncated = body[:8192]  # hard cap — see §4 context strategy
    prompt = f"From: {sender}\nSubject: {subject}\n\n{body_truncated}"

    response = await gemini_client.aio.models.generate_content(
        model="gemini-2.0-flash",
        contents=prompt,
        config=genai_types.GenerateContentConfig(
            system_instruction=CLASSIFICATION_SYSTEM_PROMPT,
            response_mime_type="application/json",
            response_json_schema=EmailClassification,   # Pydantic model → JSON schema
            safety_settings=BUSINESS_EMAIL_SAFETY,
            max_output_tokens=256,
            temperature=0.0,                            # deterministic classification
        ),
    )
    return EmailClassification.model_validate_json(response.text)

# ── Tier 2: Extract PROMO details with Claude Haiku ──────────────────────────
async def extract_promo(
    haiku_client: anthropic.AsyncAnthropic,
    haiku_semaphore: asyncio.Semaphore,
    subject: str,
    body: str,
) -> PromoDetails:
    async with haiku_semaphore:   # max 5 concurrent Haiku calls (CONTEXT.md decision)
        body_truncated = body[:8192]
        parsed = await haiku_client.messages.parse(
            model="claude-haiku-4-5-20251001",
            max_tokens=512,
            temperature=0.1,
            system=(
                "You extract structured promotional offer details from wine supplier emails. "
                "Return all monetary amounts in USD. Use ISO 8601 for dates. "
                "If a field is absent from the email, omit it (return null)."
            ),
            messages=[{
                "role": "user",
                "content": f"Subject: {subject}\n\n{body_truncated}",
            }],
            output_format=PromoDetails,    # native SDK Pydantic parsing
        )
        return parsed.parsed_output

# ── Tier 2: Generate daily digest prose with Claude Haiku ────────────────────
async def generate_digest_prose(
    haiku_client: anthropic.AsyncAnthropic,
    haiku_semaphore: asyncio.Semaphore,
    digest_data: dict,
) -> str:
    async with haiku_semaphore:
        msg = await haiku_client.messages.create(
            model="claude-haiku-4-5-20251001",
            max_tokens=512,
            system=(
                "You write concise, actionable daily briefings for a restaurant wine manager. "
                "Tone: professional but conversational. Be specific — mention vendor names, "
                "products, percentages, and dates. Keep to 3–5 sentences."
            ),
            messages=[{
                "role": "user",
                "content": (
                    f"Write a daily digest paragraph from these deals and alerts:\n"
                    f"{digest_data}"
                ),
            }],
        )
        return msg.content[0].text

# ── Orchestrator entry point ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
async def process_email_intel(
    gemini_client: genai.Client,
    haiku_client: anthropic.AsyncAnthropic,
    haiku_semaphore: asyncio.Semaphore,
    payload: dict,
) -> dict:
    subject = payload.get("subject", "")
    sender  = payload.get("from", "")
    body    = payload.get("body", "")

    # Step 1: classify (Gemini Flash — fast, cheap)
    try:
        classification = await classify_email(gemini_client, subject, sender, body)
    except Exception as e:
        # Fallback: use Haiku for classification if Gemini unavailable
        classification = await _classify_with_haiku_fallback(haiku_client, haiku_semaphore, subject, sender, body)

    result = {"category": classification.category, "confidence": classification.confidence}

    # Step 2: extract if PROMO (Haiku)
    if classification.category == EmailCategory.PROMO:
        promo = await extract_promo(haiku_client, haiku_semaphore, subject, body)
        result["promo"] = promo.model_dump(exclude_none=True)

    return result
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `genai.Client()` | Singleton connection to Gemini Developer API (API key auth) | Initialize once in `EmailIntelAgent.initialize()`; reuse across all calls |
| `client.aio.models.generate_content()` | Async variant of Gemini content generation under the new Client API (>= 1.0.0) | **All** Gemini calls in the async BaseAgent — never use the sync `client.models.generate_content()` |
| `types.GenerateContentConfig(response_json_schema=Model)` | Forces Gemini to return JSON matching a Pydantic model schema | Email classification; any Gemini call where you need a typed structured response |
| `types.SafetySetting(category=..., threshold="BLOCK_ONLY_HIGH")` | Per-category content safety filter applied before returning the response | Set for ALL four harm categories in email classification to avoid false positives on marketing language |
| `anthropic.AsyncAnthropic()` | Async Anthropic client; wraps the REST API with native asyncio support | All Haiku calls in the async agent — never use `anthropic.Anthropic()` in an async context |
| `client.messages.parse(output_format=PydanticModel)` | Native SDK structured output — converts Pydantic model to JSON schema, validates response, returns `ParsedMessage` with `.parsed_output` | PROMO extraction where schema compliance is mandatory; replaces manual `tool_use` round-trip |
| `asyncio.Semaphore(n)` | Limits concurrent coroutines to `n` at any time | Wrap every Haiku call with `async with haiku_semaphore` — created in `initialize()`, not at class instantiation |

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Old vs. new google-genai API**: The codebase (`email_parsing_agent.py`) uses the **old** pattern: `import google.generativeai as genai` → `genai.configure(api_key=...)` → `genai.GenerativeModel("gemini-pro")`. This is the `google-generativeai` package (pre-1.0 SDK). The new `google-genai >= 1.0.0` package uses `from google import genai` → `genai.Client(api_key=...)` → `client.aio.models.generate_content()`. Do **not** mix them — the module names collide and the API surface is entirely different.

2. **`response_schema` vs `response_json_schema`**: The docs show both; they are not interchangeable. `response_schema` accepts only `Enum` classes for enum-constrained output. `response_json_schema` accepts Pydantic `BaseModel` classes or raw dict schemas for full structured output. Email classification requires `response_json_schema=EmailClassification`.

3. **Safety settings false positives on promotional email content**: Marketing language ("exclusive deal", "50% off", "limited time offer") can trigger `HARM_CATEGORY_HARASSMENT` or `HARM_CATEGORY_DANGEROUS_CONTENT` at default thresholds (`BLOCK_MEDIUM_AND_ABOVE`). For business email classification, set all four categories to `BLOCK_ONLY_HIGH` to prevent legitimate PROMO emails from being blocked and misclassified as NOISE.

4. **`asyncio.Semaphore` created at wrong scope**: `asyncio.Semaphore(5)` must be created inside the running event loop (i.e., inside an `async def` function, typically `EmailIntelAgent.initialize()`). Creating it at `__init__` time or at module level in Python < 3.10 binds it to the wrong event loop, causing `RuntimeError: no running event loop` or silent double-loop issues.

5. **`messages.parse()` vs `messages.create()` for extraction**: `messages.parse()` is the correct method for Pydantic-validated structured output. `messages.create()` returns raw `Message` objects — you'd have to parse `message.content[0].text` manually, losing validation. Use `messages.parse(output_format=PromoDetails)` for extraction; use `messages.create()` only for prose generation where you want raw text.

6. **Haiku rate limits vs. semaphore size**: Claude Haiku Tier 1 limit is 50 requests/minute. The semaphore at 5 concurrent requests with typical ~2s latency yields ~150 req/min theoretical throughput — well within limits. Do **not** raise the semaphore above 10 without confirming your Anthropic tier allows it.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**

| Task | Model | Temperature | Max Output Tokens | Notes |
|------|-------|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| Email classification | `gemini-2.0-flash` | `0.0` | `256` | Deterministic; classification output is < 100 tokens |
| PROMO extraction | `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` | `0.1` | `512` | Slight warmth allows flexible field parsing; schema enforces structure |
| Digest prose | `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` | `0.4` | `512` | Higher temperature for natural prose variation across days |
| Fallback classification | `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` | `0.0` | `256` | Used when Gemini Flash fails; same schema as Gemini classification path |

**Core Pattern:**

The pipeline is a linear classification → conditional extraction fan-out, all async:

```python
import asyncio
from google import genai
from google.genai import types as genai_types
import anthropic
from core.base_agent import BaseAgent
from services.model_clients import get_gemini_client, get_haiku_client

class EmailIntelAgent(BaseAgent):
    """Classifies inbound emails and routes/extracts accordingly."""

    def __init__(self, message_bus, db, config=None):
        super().__init__(
            agent_name="email_intel_agent",
            message_bus=message_bus,
            db=db,
            config=config or {},
        )
        self.gemini_client: genai.Client | None = None
        self.haiku_client: anthropic.AsyncAnthropic | None = None
        self.haiku_semaphore: asyncio.Semaphore | None = None

    async def initialize(self) -> None:
        """Create clients + semaphore inside the running event loop."""
        self.gemini_client = get_gemini_client()           # singleton from model_clients.py
        self.haiku_client = get_haiku_client()             # singleton from model_clients.py
        self.haiku_semaphore = asyncio.Semaphore(5)        # MUST create here, not in __init__
        self.logger.info("EmailIntelAgent initialized — Gemini Flash + Haiku ready")

    def get_subscribed_routing_keys(self):
        return [("email.events", "email.inbound.received")]

    async def process_message(self, routing_key: str, payload: dict) -> None:
        if routing_key != "email.inbound.received":
            return

        label_ids = payload.get("labelIds", [])
        direction = "inbound" if "INBOX" in label_ids else "outbound"
        if direction == "outbound":
            await self._link_sent_email(payload)
            return

        result = await self._run_classification_pipeline(payload)
        await self._dispatch(payload, result)

    async def _run_classification_pipeline(self, payload: dict) -> dict:
        subject = payload.get("subject", "")
        sender  = payload.get("from", "")
        body    = payload.get("body", "")[:8192]   # always truncate before ANY call

        try:
            classification = await classify_email(
                self.gemini_client, subject, sender, body
            )
        except Exception as exc:
            self.logger.warning(f"Gemini Flash failed ({exc}); falling back to Haiku")
            classification = await _classify_with_haiku_fallback(
                self.haiku_client, self.haiku_semaphore, subject, sender, body
            )

        category = classification.category
        result = {
            "category": category,
            "confidence": classification.confidence,
            "reasoning": classification.reasoning,
        }

        if category == "PROMO":
            promo = await extract_promo(
                self.haiku_client, self.haiku_semaphore, subject, body
            )
            result["promo"] = promo.model_dump(exclude_none=True)

        return result

    async def _dispatch(self, payload: dict, result: dict) -> None:
        category = result["category"]
        restaurant_id = payload.get("restaurant_id")

        if category == "OPERATIONAL":
            await self.message_bus.publish(
                "email.events", "email.inbound.received",
                {**payload, "__intel_bypass": True}
            )
            await self._create_notification(restaurant_id, "operational", payload)

        elif category == "PROMO":
            promo_data = result.get("promo", {})
            await self._store_vendor_promotion(restaurant_id, payload, promo_data)
            await self._accumulate_digest(restaurant_id, promo_data)
            await self._create_notification(restaurant_id, "promo", payload, promo_data)
            await self._link_calendar_events(restaurant_id, promo_data)
        # NOISE: silent drop
```

**Tool Use:**

- **Gemini Flash**: No tool use. Structured output via `response_json_schema=EmailClassification` + `response_mime_type="application/json"` in `GenerateContentConfig`. Single-turn, no round-trip needed.
- **Haiku extraction**: Uses `client.messages.parse(output_format=PromoDetails)` — SDK natively converts Pydantic model to `json_schema` output format. No manual `tool_use` round-trip.
- **Haiku prose (digest)**: Uses `client.messages.create()` — plain text output; no structured output needed.
- **Haiku fallback classification**: Use `messages.parse(output_format=EmailClassification)` — same schema as Gemini path; always validated.

**State Management:**

| Store | Key / Schema | What Lives There | TTL |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|-----|
| Redis | `digest:{restaurant_id}:{date}` (list) | Accumulated PROMO deal summaries for nightly digest | 36 hours |
| Supabase `vendor_promotions` | One row per PROMO; `dedup_hash = vendor_email + product_name + date` | Full structured PROMO data; `urgency_score`, `linked_event_ids[]` | Permanent |
| Supabase `procurement_conversations` | `gmail_thread_id` FK | Full email thread with `direction` field (inbound/outbound) | Permanent |
| In-process | `self.haiku_semaphore` | Concurrency gate for Haiku calls | Agent lifetime |

EmailIntelAgent is **stateless per message** — each email is classified independently with no rolling context.

**Context Window Strategy:**

- **Email body truncation**: Slice `body[:8192]` before building any prompt. Subject + sender add < 200 tokens. Total classification prompt: ≈ 500–4,500 tokens — well within Gemini Flash's 1M context and Haiku's 200K context.
- **Digest accumulation**: Each Redis list entry ≤ 200 characters. At 20% PROMO hit rate on 1,000 emails/day → ≤ 200 entries → ≤ 40,000 characters. If the list exceeds 100 items, truncate to the top 20 by `urgency_score` before the Haiku prose call.
- **Digest prose input budget**: Target ≤ 1,500 input tokens. Set `max_tokens=512` for prose output — 3–5 sentences never exceeds this.

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

**Output models for this system** — define these in a shared `models/email_intel.py`:

```python
# services/agent-orchestrator/models/email_intel.py
from __future__ import annotations
from enum import Enum
from typing import Optional
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field, field_validator

class EmailCategory(str, Enum):
    OPERATIONAL = "OPERATIONAL"
    PROMO = "PROMO"
    NOISE = "NOISE"

class EmailClassification(BaseModel):
    """Output schema for Gemini Flash classification AND Haiku fallback classification."""
    category: EmailCategory
    confidence: float = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0, description="Model confidence in the category")
    reasoning: str = Field(max_length=200, description="One-sentence explanation")

    @field_validator("confidence")
    @classmethod
    def round_confidence(cls, v: float) -> float:
        return round(v, 3)

class PromoDetails(BaseModel):
    """Structured extraction output for PROMO emails — used by Haiku extraction pass."""
    product_name: str = Field(description="Wine product name, e.g. '2022 Burgundy Premier Cru'")
    vendor_name: str = Field(description="Supplier/vendor company name")
    discount_percent: Optional[float] = Field(
        default=None, ge=0.0, le=100.0,
        description="Discount as a percentage, e.g. 20.0 for 20% off"
    )
    original_price_usd: Optional[float] = Field(default=None, ge=0.0)
    discounted_price_usd: Optional[float] = Field(default=None, ge=0.0)
    validity_date: Optional[str] = Field(
        default=None, description="Offer expiry in ISO 8601 format: YYYY-MM-DD"
    )
    wine_variety: Optional[str] = Field(
        default=None, description="Grape variety or wine type, e.g. 'Pinot Noir'"
    )
    quantity_available: Optional[str] = Field(
        default=None, description="Available quantity if mentioned, e.g. '48 cases'"
    )
    minimum_order: Optional[str] = Field(default=None)

class DigestPromo(BaseModel):
    """Compact PROMO summary stored in Redis digest list (≤ 200 chars when serialized)."""
    vendor: str
    product: str
    discount_pct: Optional[float] = None
    price_usd: Optional[float] = None
    expiry: Optional[str] = None
    urgency_score: float = Field(default=0.0, ge=0.0, le=10.0)
```

| Model | Integration Method | How Validation Works |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|

**Retry logic:**

```python
async def extract_promo_with_retry(
    haiku_client: anthropic.AsyncAnthropic,
    semaphore: asyncio.Semaphore,
    subject: str,
    body: str,
    max_retries: int = 2,
) -> PromoDetails:
    last_error = None
    for attempt in range(max_retries + 1):
        async with semaphore:
            try:
                parsed = await haiku_client.messages.parse(
                    model="claude-haiku-4-5-20251001",
                    max_tokens=512,
                    temperature=0.1 if attempt == 0 else 0.0,  # cooler on retry
                    system=(
                        "Extract structured promotional offer details. "
                        "If a field is absent, return null. "
                        + ("Be strictly literal — do not infer or assume values." if attempt > 0 else "")
                    ),
                    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": f"Subject: {subject}\n\n{body}"}],
                    output_format=PromoDetails,
                )
                return parsed.parsed_output
            except anthropic.BadRequestError as exc:
                # Schema validation failed — log and retry with stricter prompt
                logger.warning(f"PromoDetails parse failed (attempt {attempt+1}): {exc}")
                last_error = exc
            except anthropic.APIStatusError as exc:
                if exc.status_code == 529:   # overloaded
                    await asyncio.sleep(2 ** attempt)
                    last_error = exc
                else:
                    raise
    # All retries exhausted — return minimal safe fallback
    logger.error(f"PromoDetails extraction failed after {max_retries+1} attempts: {last_error}")
    return PromoDetails(product_name="Unknown", vendor_name="Unknown")
```

**The one common mistake** — creating `asyncio.Semaphore` at class instantiation:
```python

class EmailIntelAgent(BaseAgent):
    def __init__(self, ...):
        self.haiku_semaphore = asyncio.Semaphore(5)   # RuntimeError risk in Python < 3.10

# CORRECT — created in initialize(), which runs inside the running event loop
    async def initialize(self) -> None:
        self.haiku_semaphore = asyncio.Semaphore(5)   # safe
```

**Stream vs. await:**
- **Use `await` (non-streaming)** for classification and structured extraction — you need the complete, validated response before acting on it.
- **Use streaming** only for the digest prose generation if you want to show the digest building up in a UI. For the automated DigestCronJob sending an email, `await messages.create()` is simpler and sufficient.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

**System vs. user prompt separation** — always kept separate in both SDKs:

```python
# Gemini: system_instruction in GenerateContentConfig, user content in contents=
config = genai_types.GenerateContentConfig(
    system_instruction="You are an email triage agent...",  # fixed across all calls
    ...
)
response = await client.aio.models.generate_content(
    model="gemini-2.0-flash",
    contents=f"From: {sender}\nSubject: {subject}\n\n{body}",  # per-message
    config=config,
)

# Anthropic: system= param, user message in messages=
await client.messages.parse(
    model="claude-haiku-4-5-20251001",
    system="You extract structured promotional offer details...",  # fixed
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": email_content}],       # per-message
    ...
)
```

**Few-shot guidance:**
- Classification: embed 2 inline examples in the system prompt (one OPERATIONAL, one PROMO). Do NOT use dynamic retrieval — examples are static and always relevant.
- Extraction: NO few-shot needed. Pydantic schema + clear field descriptions are sufficient for Haiku.
- Digest prose: provide the desired tone via a single example sentence in the system prompt ("e.g., Yesterday, 3 deals arrived. Most relevant: ..."). One shot is enough.

**Token budget rules (non-negotiable):**
- Never leave `max_output_tokens` (Gemini) or `max_tokens` (Anthropic) unbounded in production.
- Classification: 256 tokens. Extraction: 512 tokens. Prose: 512 tokens.
- Email body: always truncated to ≤ 8,192 characters before any call.

### Context Window Management

**Email body truncation — slice before pipeline entry, not inside each function:**

**Digest accumulation — Redis list as context buffer:**
- Each `DigestPromo` serializes to ≤ 200 characters (JSON). Cap list at 100 items via Redis `LTRIM digest:{id}:{date} 0 99` after each `RPUSH`.
- At digest send time: fetch full list, sort by `urgency_score` descending, take top 20 if > 20 items.
- Pass formatted list to Haiku; expected input ≤ 4,000 tokens for 20 deals + stalled threads + procurement gaps.

**Conversation history for ProviderConversationAgent (existing agent):**
- CONTEXT.md D-19: inject last 3 interactions from `procurement_conversations` into Haiku prompt for draft generation.
- Pre-format: retrieve 3 rows, format as `[{role, snippet, date}]`, max 1,200 tokens. Truncate older messages first.

### Cost and Latency Budget

**Per-call cost estimates (2026 pricing):**

| Operation | Model | Input tokens | Output tokens | Cost/call | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------------|---------------|-----------|-------|
| Classification | Gemini 2.0 Flash | ~500 | ~80 | ~$0.000061 | $0.075/1M in, $0.30/1M out |
| PROMO extraction | Haiku 4.5 | ~600 | ~200 | ~$0.000400 | $0.25/1M in, $1.25/1M out |
| Digest prose | Haiku 4.5 | ~1,500 | ~350 | ~$0.000813 | 1 call/restaurant/day |
| Fallback classification | Haiku 4.5 | ~500 | ~80 | ~$0.000225 | Only on Gemini failure |

**Daily cost at scale (single restaurant, 1,000 emails/day, 20% PROMO):**
- Classification: 1,000 × $0.000061 = **$0.061/day**
- PROMO extraction: 200 × $0.000400 = **$0.080/day**
- Digest prose: 1 × $0.000813 = **$0.001/day**
- **Total: ~$0.14/day → ~$4.30/month per restaurant**

**Caching and deduplication strategy:**
- **`dedup_hash`**: Before calling Haiku for PROMO extraction, check Supabase `vendor_promotions` for an existing row with `dedup_hash = SHA256(vendor_email + product_name + date)`. If found, skip the Haiku call entirely. This is the primary cost control mechanism.
- **No response caching for classification**: Each email is unique; exact-match caching has near-zero hit rate.
- **Digest prose**: DigestCronJob runs once per restaurant per day. The prose is never regenerated until the next day. No caching needed — single call by design.

**Model routing decisions:**
- Gemini Flash (Tier 1) → **all** classification calls. $0.075/1M input is 3.3× cheaper than Haiku ($0.25/1M). At 1,000 emails/day, this saves $0.19/day vs. using Haiku for everything.
- Haiku (Tier 2) → extraction + prose only. Haiku's instruction-following for structured extraction is measurably more reliable than Gemini Flash for multi-field nullable schemas.
- Gemini Flash fallback to Haiku: triggered only on `Exception` from the Gemini call, not on low-confidence classifications. Low confidence (< 0.6) is a separate signal — log it and route to NOISE as a safe default.
- Never use a larger model (Sonnet, Opus) in this pipeline — the tasks are well-defined enough that Haiku is ceiling.

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger Condition | Intervention | Latency Budget |
|-----------|------------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Commitment Language Block** | Any draft reply on the `auto_reply_enabled` path matches patterns: `we'll take \d+`, `please confirm our order`, `we'll proceed`, `we'd like to order`, `confirm.*cases`, `accept.*price`, or equivalent price/quantity acceptance language | Block auto-send; set conversation to `pending_approval`; append `⚠️ Commitment language detected — manager review required` flag to the approval card | <50ms (pure regex, no LLM call) |
| **OPERATIONAL Silence Prevention** | Gemini Flash returns `confidence < 0.60` on any inbound email — uncertain classification creates silent OPERATIONAL drop risk | Override: route as OPERATIONAL regardless of predicted category; publish to EmailParsingAgent; set `low_confidence: true` in decision_log; queue for weekly human review batch | <10ms (threshold check in _dispatch()) |
| **Extraction Fabrication Warning** | Any `PromoDetails` numeric field (price, discount %, quantity) does not appear as a substring in the source email body after whitespace normalization | Null out the suspicious field; annotate `vendor_promotions` row with `extraction_warning: "value not found in source"`; surface `⚠️ Verify price with vendor` badge on the deal card — do not block the record | <100ms (text substring match, no LLM) |
| **Tenant Isolation Assertion** | Any DB write to `vendor_promotions`, `procurement_conversations`, or notification creation where `restaurant_id` does not match the authenticated session context | Block the write; log a security event to Langfuse with full payload; route the message to DLQ for investigation | <5ms (application-level assertion before every write) |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|-----------------------|
| **PROMO Classification Drift** | 100% of classification results logged to Langfuse with `category + confidence + email_subject`; 5% randomly sampled weekly for human re-labeling | If sampled PROMO recall drops below 95%: P1 alert → engineering reviews prompt and model version; hold next agent deploy |
| **OPERATIONAL Recall Drift** | Same Langfuse log; all emails with `confidence < 0.85` automatically queued for weekly human review | If sampled OPERATIONAL recall drops below 98%: P0 alert → immediate investigation; consider lowering OPERATIONAL silence guardrail threshold to 0.70 |
| **Urgency Score Inflation** | Weekly distribution query on `vendor_promotions.urgency_score` per restaurant for rolling 7-day window; logged as a Langfuse score metric | If >30% of deals score ≥8/10 in any 7-day window: flag for beverage director spot-check and urgency composite formula audit |
| **Reply Tone Quality Trend** | 5% random sample of all approved and auto-sent drafts scored weekly by LLM judge (calibrated vs. ops manager rubric); scores logged to Langfuse | If average tone score drops below 3.5/5 over 2 consecutive weekly batches: trigger human review sprint — send 20 drafts to sommelier + ops manager for rating; audit Haiku tone prompt |
| **Digest Hallucination Check** | Every digest output compared to its Redis input before send: verify all vendor names and numeric values in prose exist verbatim in the accumulated deal data | If any mismatch detected: withhold digest; re-generate with `temperature=0.0` and stricter system prompt ("only reference facts explicitly present in the input"); alert engineering |
| **Extraction Fabrication Rate** | Count of `extraction_warning` flags per 100 PROMO emails, logged weekly in Langfuse | If fabrication warning rate exceeds 2% of PROMO emails: review Haiku extraction prompt; add failing cases to adversarial reference dataset; tighten field descriptions in `PromoDetails` |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Langfuse (self-hosted via Docker Compose alongside agent-orchestrator)

Instrument with `@observe()` decorator on: `classify_email()`, `extract_promo()`, `generate_digest_prose()`, and `ProviderConversationAgent.generate_draft()`. Log `restaurant_id`, `email_subject`, `category`, `confidence`, `promo_fields_extracted`, and `draft_word_count` as metadata on every trace. Register all system prompts in Langfuse Prompt Management and pin to versioned IDs — prompt changes must be auditable and reversible without a code deploy.

**Key Metrics to Track:**

| Metric | Source | Alert Condition |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| PROMO recall (sampled weekly) | Human re-labeling of 5% weekly sample logged in Langfuse | <95% → P1 alert |
| OPERATIONAL recall (sampled weekly) | Human re-labeling of 5% weekly sample; 100% of low-confidence (<0.85) emails | <98% → P0 alert |
| Commitment language guardrail trigger rate | Langfuse tag count: `commitment_language_blocked` / total auto-reply drafts | >5% in any week → P2 alert (possible prompt regression) |
| Gemini Flash p95 classification latency | Langfuse trace durations, percentile query | >3,000ms p95 → P2 alert; >5,000ms → investigate Haiku fallback path |
| Daily digest delivery rate | DigestCronJob success/skip/failure events logged to Langfuse | >10% of configured restaurants miss digest by 8:30am → P2 alert |
| Haiku extraction fallback rate | Count of `PromoDetails(product_name="Unknown")` per 100 PROMO emails | >2% → P2 alert (schema drift or Anthropic API issue) |

**Alert Thresholds:**

| Severity | Condition | Response |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| P0 (immediate, on-call) | OPERATIONAL recall <98% in weekly sample | Revert to last known-good prompt version in Langfuse; manual OPERATIONAL routing until resolved |
| P1 (same business day) | PROMO recall <95% in weekly sample | Engineering audit of prompt version and model; freeze new agent deployments |
| P2 (next business day) | Guardrail trigger rate >5%, p95 latency >5s, digest failure >10%, extraction fallback >2% | Investigate in next sprint; add failing cases to reference dataset |
| P3 (weekly review cycle) | Urgency score inflation >30% of deals ≥8/10, tone trend score <3.5/5 for 2 consecutive weeks | Beverage director spot-check + Haiku prompt audit in next planning cycle |

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**

| Signal | Sample Rate | Routing |
|--------|------------|---------|
| `confidence < 0.75` on any classification | 100% | Human review queue in Langfuse; weekly batch reviewed by ops team |
| Commitment language guardrail triggered | 100% | Ops manager review within 24h; confirmed false positive → add pattern to allowlist |
| Manager edits a draft before sending | 100% | Log original + edited diff to Langfuse as `correction` event; priority samples for tone rubric calibration and `manager_instructions` learning loop (D-12) |
| `extraction_warning` flag on any PromoDetails record | 100% | Engineer review within 1 week; confirmed fabrication → add to adversarial test dataset |
| PROMO with `urgency_score ≥ 8` that manager dismisses without action | 100% | Log dismissal signal; feed into urgency score calibration review sprint |
| Random baseline (no signal present) | 1% | Unbiased baseline for coverage tracking; prevents signal-only sampling from masking systemic drift |
