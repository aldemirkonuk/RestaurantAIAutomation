Mudavym · /cellar · fourth pass, 2026-09-03

A and B, merged.

The founder: “Both the direction A and B look great in their own fields, research on it try to merge those two.” They are not alternatives to each other — they answer different questions. A answers where. B answers everything, at once. The shipped book answers this kind, in full. The merge keeps all three and pays each one’s cost only where it is small.

The three screens

1 · The parent

The adaptive name, the four info boxes the founder asked to keep, A’s floor reduced to a strip of zones that carry a real count, the registers, and B’s “everything at once” with the general column set.

2 · A register

The per-register columns, the column’s own menu on right-click, the series a cell opens (graph then ledger), and the four columns not drawn with the measurement that says why.

3 · A row, opened

The /inventory dropdown shape on a cellar row, in place, both grounds — including the two cards this register cannot fill, drawn hatched with their reason instead of dropped.

What each direction contributed

A · the cellar floor

Contributed: the room, and the only answer on the whole page to where — plus the two verbs that come with it (walk a zone, put away what is at the door). Cost, and how the merge pays it: A stands on storage_locations, which holds 87 rows across 7 tenants with 84 carrying one of four invented zone names (measured on production 2026-09-02). Drawn as the whole parent it is a picture of fabricated rooms. Reduced to a strip, and drawn only from zones that carry a real count and a real walk date, it costs nothing and still answers where.

B · one register, kind as a facet

Contributed: “see everything” taken literally — a whisky, a keg and a Burgundy in one list, with the register as a facet across the top. Cost, and how the merge pays it: one row grammar for three kinds of thing, which is the thing the four-child IA was chosen to avoid. The merge pays it only for the eight columns that survive being general (Bottle · Register · Our record · On the list · Paid · Sold · Taken · Last quote), and refuses the ninth: On hand is real for wines and structurally absent for the other six, so as a general column it would be an em dash on most of the page.

The shipped book

Contributed: the spine. A parent of registers, each opening at full breadth, with depth off the row. It is the only one of the three that needed nothing further, and both of the others now hang off it rather than replacing it.

The one thing the merge adds that neither direction had

A gesture set, stated once. The header owns the column — right-click it or press its caret for sort, hide, and the column’s own account of itself (what it means, which table it reads, how much of it is filled). The row owns its record — click it and it opens in place as cards. The cell owns the series — double-click or right-click and the graph and the ledger behind that one figure open below. That split is not invented here: Airtable’s field-header menu is schema and nothing else, TradingView says outright that “you can click any row to open the chart” while a right-click on a column header customises the column, and Bloomberg has answered “graph or table?” with both since the terminal shipped — GP draws the series, HP tables it.
