Mudavym. The Ledger

The house's book of record. Warm paper, honest ink, one seal. Every number can show its working; what we don't know renders as "—", never as a pass.

§01Logo — three marks for the book

A name you sign, not a logo you launch

All three concepts treat the wordmark as an entry in the book: serif, quiet, final. The mark is whatever the house uses to close an entry — a total rule, a seal, a full stop.

1 · The Double RuleThe accountant's mark under a final total. The brand promise in two hairlines: this number is done, checked, and stands. Mark variant: "M" over the double rule.
2 · The SealA wax impression: entered, approved, sealed. Doubles as the product's approval stamp — the same mark that lands on a purchase order lives on the door. The stitched inner ring reads as thread, hand-bound.
3 · The Full Stop"Mudavym." — the sentence ends here. The burgundy period is the entire identity system: it closes the wordmark, dots the app tile, and ends every verdict the product writes. Smallest possible mark, hardest to forget.
§02Slogans — candidates for the record

Seven candidates

All follow the reference grammar: balanced antithesis, or name-period-claim. No adjectives praise the product; the record does.

§03Voice — how the book writes

The ledger never flatters, never hides

Deadpan brand, quoted warmth: Mudavym's own copy stays plain and exact; warmth enters through the humans it quotes — vendors, staff, the AI's drafted letters.

  • Say what's known; mark what isn't.Unknowns render as "—", never 0, never a filled bar. An empty cell is information.
  • Every figure can show its working.Any number the product asserts must open into its arithmetic. If it can't, it isn't asserted — it's estimated, and says so.
  • Verbs first, numbers second, adjectives never."Approve 12 bottles — $1,860" beats "Smart reorder suggestion!". The brand never praises itself.
  • Serif is the product speaking — once per screen.The greeting, the verdict, the question. Everything else is working text in sans and mono.
  • Plain trade words, no software words."Bottles in", "close the night", "on the record" — not SKUs, syncing, or workflows. The reader runs a restaurant.
  • Ask before acting; record after acting.The AI drafts, the human enters. Every action gets a line: who, what, when. Nothing auto-sends.
  • Warmth arrives quoted.Vendor replies, staff notes, the drafted letter's own courtesy — shown in italics, attributed. The brand around them stays deadpan.
  • Endings are spoken slowly and plainly.Deletion and closure copy states exactly what is lost, in full sentences, with no dark patterns and no euphemism.

§04Palette — paper, ink, one seal

Neutrals do 95% of the work

Warm paper with a four-step ink ladder; surfaces are alpha tints of warm ink, never painted gray. One burgundy seal — it earned its place through the cellar. Every status hue ships with its tint twin, so state color never sits raw on the page. Dark mode is lamplight, not gray: near-black biased warm.

§06Motion — the hand that writes settles

Deliberate, settling, never bouncy

Ink dries; it doesn't wobble. Everything lands on fast-out/long-settle curves in the 140–420ms band. One emphatic exception: the stamp — approvals land with a touch of overshoot, because entering something into the book should feel like a small ceremony.
