097 · Where the outward connections live.

The founder's note on /profile: “be definite about comprehensiveness of design, MCP's to connectors, to third-party apps. Maybe not in profile you're right.”

You are right. Three of /profile's seven registers are about the house, not the person — and one of them is the house's cards on file, on a page every member reaches.

The rule the field agrees on, from ten products: placement follows whose credential it is and in whose name the action is taken, never what the thing is called. A credential that authenticates a person and whose acts are attributed to that person is personal. A credential that authenticates the business and whose acts are attributed to the business belongs to the house, behind a role gate. Where both are true — the org permits an app, each person connects their own account — every one of them builds two tiers with a governance link, and never one.

The recommendation, definitely: a house-scoped surface called Connections, opening on the line “What acts for this house”, holding the till, the payment provider, the sender identity, the calendar feed, the public page and the model-context servers — plus a named-but-not-revocable row for every personal grant that acts inside the house. /profile keeps who you are, what protects you, and what is attached to you. No page note is created for this; it is described in profile.md §13a and the parent decides the route.

What only the founder can decide

  1. Does Connections get its own route, or a Settings section? Toast and Zapier put it top-level because connections are the substance of the product; Notion, Slack, Square and Linear make it a settings section because they are not. The founder's own words this pass — “we need to keep the customer inside the app… so MCP or API connections is a must” — argue for top-level. The cost is one nav row, role-gated so staff never see it.
  2. Whose is a model-context server? The table's comment says it “acts with the user's authority”; the register calls them “servers the house agents may call”. Today a row is keyed to one person and one restaurant, so the owner cannot see the GM's server and deleting the GM deletes the house's Toast bridge. Claude, Notion, Slack and Claude Code all answer: the house declares, the person consents.
  3. May a model-context tool ever be called, and what is the human step? The seal, a draft to approve, or a per-tool grant. One server in the sketch advertises place_order. ADR 0013 has never been extended to this.
  4. Does the house get its own sending address? Every vendor letter leaves from one mailbox the whole deployment shares, with notifications@wineops.ai as the hardcoded fallback. Fixing it is a domain, a DNS record and a provider decision — not a page.
  5. Can a manager see what a member has connected? A GM's personal Drive grant currently receives the house's exports and no owner can see that it exists. Naming it on Connections is the minimum; requiring approval before it is made is the stronger answer, and it is a policy call.
