Mudavym

I have read your fourteen registers. Eleven of them are still holding column defaults, which means nobody has answered them — not that anyone chose them. Three you have already answered, and I will leave those alone unless you tell me otherwise.

Why it opens this way. A default is an answer nobody gave. The house already refuses to read lead_time_days = 7 as a stated term (vendor-terms.service.ts:47–52), so the assistant opens with the same distinction rather than presenting eleven blanks as eleven settings.

Aldemir

We’re a small meyhane. Forty covers. Closed Mondays, and we run late — last order goes in about half one.

Mudavym

Then your service day should not settle at midnight. I would close the books six hours after the last order rather than on the calendar date, and hold Monday out of the baselines so a closed day does not read as a bad day.

Notifications → settle window Calendar → Mondays excluded

Two registers moved from one sentence. This is the thing an interview cannot do.

Aldemir

I buy from Anadolu, Ege Šarap and Kadıköy Bira. Anadolu wants the order in by eleven or it goes to the next van. I sign anything over five thousand lira myself; Deniz can do the rest.

Mudavym

Recorded as four things. Anadolu’s cutoff at 11:00 — stated by you, not inferred, so it will read as a stated term. Ege and Kadıköy I have no cutoff for, and I am not going to guess one; they will stay unanswered until somebody says. The ceiling is ₺5,000 with the owner signing above it, and Deniz stays a manager.

Vendor terms → Anadolu cutoff Approval thresholds → ceiling Approval thresholds → role

The refusal is the point. Two of three vendors have no cutoff. Filling them with Anadolu’s would be the exact fault the vendor-terms register was built to stop.

Aldemir

Don’t ring my phone during service. And tell me when a wine gets cheap.

Mudavym

The first I can do: quiet hours 18:00–01:30, everything held and delivered in one digest at 10:00. The second I cannot set for you, and I would rather say so than draw you a control that goes nowhere. The price-drop threshold is one number for every house on this deployment — it is read from MARKET_SIGNAL_DROP_PCT, not from your registers, and it is at 10% today. Your alerts will fire at 10% below the thirty-day average whether you want that or not.

Notifications → quiet hours

Measured, not hedged. market-price.producer.ts:95–97 reads the value from configuration; the default is 0.1 at market-signal.ts:93. There is no per-restaurant column to write, so there is no control — and the assistant names the number rather than leaving a silence the owner would read as agreement.

Aldemir

We do wine, beer and rakı. No cocktails, no whiskey list.

Mudavym

That contradicts something a person already answered. On 21 August somebody on this account turned the whiskey register on. I am not going to quietly switch it off inside a batch — I have put it in a separate section for you to decide on its own.

Cellar → wine, beer, rakı Cellar → whiskey: contested, not batched

The batch only ever fills silence. A register already holding a stated value never enters it. Contesting one is its own single decision with its own seal — which is what keeps the seal at the bottom of the batch meaningful.

Mudavym

That is eight values. Six I can write for this restaurant, one is kept in your browser and I will apply it here rather than on the server, and one I have already told you I cannot set. Nothing has been written. Read the list before you seal it — every row says where the current value came from, and you can drop any of them.

02

The four things the conversation is doing that a chat window does not

each one is a rule, not a nicety
The behaviourWhere you see it aboveThe rule behind it
It reads before it asks The opening turn: eleven unanswered, three answered. Without a provenance state on every register the assistant cannot tell a default from an answer, and would batch over a person. That is why settings.registers returns value + provenance, not just value.
One sentence moves several registers “Closed Mondays, we run late” → two registers. This is the entire argument against a guided interview. Fourteen questions cannot compress; one sentence can.
It refuses out loud The market-price turn, and the two vendors with no cutoff. A control whose backend does not exist is never drawn as a working control. Naming the number and its source is more useful than a slider that writes nowhere.
It contests rather than overwrites The whiskey register, moved out of the batch. The batch is only ever a batch of answers nobody gave. A stated value gets its own section and its own seal, which is what stops one gesture covering forty fields.

What the owner said, in full: six sentences. What the assistant produced: eight values, two refusals and one contest. Not one register was written.
