The restaurant's numbers, taken seriously.

01

Logo

Three marks, one wordmark. Butt-capped strokes, no curves that don't earn their keep. Every concept survives at 16px, engraves on a receipt printer, and stamps in one color.

A — THE TALLY

Four strokes and the strike

A tally of five. Müdavim is the guest you can count on — and the instrument counts. Bottles, covers, dollars, visits: the oldest ledger mark there is, cut at radius 0. The strike doubles as the check-off: a decision made.

B — THE REGISTER

Calibration corners, solid center

A registration mark: four crop corners holding one filled square. The house, centered and calibrated. Reads as a viewfinder over the operation — everything inside the corners is measured. Strong favicon at any size.

C — THE METER

An M read off a level meter

Five bars hung from the top rail spell the M as a meter readout. At rest the mark is all ink; when the product has something to say, exactly one bar carries the signal amber — the logo itself obeys the chroma-as-anomaly rule. This is the animated/app-icon variant; A and B are the print-stable ones.

WORDMARK — TWO REGISTERS

Primary: Geist 600, −0.025em — the name as a product label. Secondary: Geist Mono 500, +0.5em tracked caps — the stamp register, for receipts, exports, printed POs, and the top bar. The mono register is the one customers' accountants will see; it should look like it came off an instrument.

02

Slogans

All candidates obey the voice: no adjectives, no exclamation, structures borrowed from the spec-sheet school — "Name. Category claim." and the balanced antithesis.

S-01
Count on it.
The tally and the trust in three words. Müdavim is the guest who is counted on to return; the instrument is what the owner counts on. Both meanings are literal.
S-02
The house, measured.
"The house" is hospitality's own word for itself; "measured" is the instrument's whole promise. Two nouns, one comma, zero adjectives.
S-03
Mudavym. The restaurant's instrument.
The "Name. Category claim." structure — states the direction itself as the positioning. Works as the one-line footer on every export.
S-04
Every bottle. Every dollar. Every night.
A tally cadence — three counted nouns, full scope claim (inventory, money, service) without a single qualifier. The rhythm is the brand.
S-05
The back office, calibrated.
Names the enemy (the chaotic back office every operator knows) and applies the instrument verb to it. Best for the procurement/receiving story.
S-06
Trust the readout.
The operator's actual relationship with the product: money decisions made on instrument data. Doubles as an internal design principle — the readout must be trustworthy enough to deserve the line.
03

Voice

The product reports. It does not chat, apologize, or celebrate. Warmth exists in Mudavym — but it is always a quoted human, never the brand's own copy.

R-01

Numbers, not adjectives

"Fast" is banned; "1.8 s" is not. Any claim that matters carries a figure. A sentence that needs an adjective needs a number instead.

R-02

Every numeral is mono, tabular

Never spelled out. "7 items", not "seven items". Figures align in columns even inside prose — the typography is part of the voice.

R-03

State first, action second

The readout precedes the button. Empty states, errors, and prompts all open with the current state as a measurement, then name the one action.

R-04

Name the consequence, never ask for feelings

"Deletes 3,412 orders. No recovery." replaces "Are you sure?" The user decides on facts, not on our nervousness.

R-05

No apologies, no cheers

Never "Sorry, something went wrong!" and never "Success!" An instrument reports the state and what restores it. No exclamation marks exist in this product.

R-06

One sentence per message

A second sentence is a second message. Fragments are legal and encouraged: "0 checks." is a complete report.

R-07

Physical verbs only

Draft, approve, close, count, receive, deliver. Never manage, leverage, empower, streamline. If a hand can't do it, the verb is wrong.

R-08

Warmth is quoted, not authored

Vendor replies, staff notes, and the AI's drafts render in full human register — quoted, attributed. The chrome around them stays deadpan. The contrast is the brand.

06

Motion personality

Nothing bounces — money doesn't bounce. Three named tokens, all fast-out settle, 120–240ms. State flips snap like a switch; reveals unmask like a panel opening; numbers roll like an odometer and land exactly. Reduced-motion collapses everything to instant state change.

Token
Curve · duration
Applies to

cubic-bezier(.2, 0, 0, 1) · 120ms
Binary state: toggles, checks, chips, tab indicators. A switch, not a slide.

cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1) · 180ms
Hover, press, focus, 2–4px moves, fades. The house curve for everything small.

cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1) · 240ms
Row expands, drawers, detail panels — unmasked via clip/grid, never slid in from off-screen.

ease-out settle · 500ms
Number tickers on KPI readouts. Rolls, decelerates, lands on the exact figure.
