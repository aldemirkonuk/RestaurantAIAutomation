Mudavym · The Guest Book

The house remembers. Your table, your vintage, the vendor who shorts you every July, the server who covers every Friday. Recognition is not a feature here — it is the product.

§01Premise — recognition as the product

Every great restaurant's superpower is that it remembers

Ops software is anonymous by construction: it leads with an ID, it greets no one, and it forgets the moment a record closes. The maître d's book does the opposite — it opens on a name, in a confident hand, with the date beside it and every previous visit still on the page. Mudavym is that book, kept perfectly. The hospitality is the greeting; the technical superiority is the recall — nothing forgotten, every pattern noticed, history always one gesture away.

  • Guest-book rules and ruled linesHairline rules and a violet vertical margin rule are the structure. No cards floating on nothing; entries sit on a page.
  • Name-first hierarchyThe proper name is always the largest type in the row. The identifier is demoted to a mono caption. See §06 — this is the typographic thesis.
  • Dates and returns as a visual systemA fixed left rail carries the date, the ordinal ("9th order"), and a tally of returns drawn as pen strokes. Frequency becomes a shape you read at a glance.
  • Exactly three hand-quality marksThe signature stroke, the tally mark, the marginal note. Drawn as SVG paths — never a handwriting typeface — and held against precise modern type everywhere else.
What this direction refuses Nostalgia kitsch, script fonts, sepia, torn-paper textures, quill iconography, and any "vintage" filter over the interface. The book is current: today's page, in today's type. Warmth comes from being known, not from looking old.

§02Logo — three resolved marks

A name on a line, a count of returns, a ribbon in a page

Three complete systems, each with its app tile and its wordmark lockup, each drawn in the same ink. All three do a job inside the product, not only on the door — that is the test each one had to pass.

Concept 1The Ruled Name The wordmark is signed onto the line, not set above it — the descender of the y crosses the rule and knocks a clean break in it, exactly as a real signature crosses ruled paper. That break is the whole mark; it cannot be faked by a logo that merely sits on a bar. The rule runs past the name on both sides (the line is longer than any one signature) and closes on a violet check made across it — one of the three hand marks, never a font glyph. Product role: the rule is the same hairline that underlines every name in the app; the check is the "seen and entered" mark. Clear space = the cap height of the M. Min size 96px wide; below that, the tile alone.
Concept 2The Return Marks Four strokes and the fifth across them: the oldest count in the world, and the only glyph that means "this has happened again". The fifth stroke is gilt — the visit being recorded right now. It is a mark, not a picture: it survives 16px, engraves on brass, and stencils on a crate. Product role: the identical glyph is the return-tally in every entry rail (see §01), so the logo is literally a component of the UI — the strongest possible argument that recognition is the product. Tile lifts to #7A3A80 on dark ground so the plum still reads.
Concept 3The Kept Page A ribbon marker running out past the bottom edge of the page — the object that means "come back to this one". The fold at the page edge is a second, darker plum so the ribbon reads as material rather than a stripe. The lockup shows the page and its gutter rule; the tile is the same ribbon cropped, and the overhang is what makes it legible at any size. Product role: the ribbon is the regulars marker — it hangs on the vendor you always reorder from, the staff member who never misses, the table that is always theirs. Never rotate it, never curl it; the swallowtail notch is the only shape it has.

Recommendation: Concept 2 as the mark, Concept 1 as the wordmark — the tally is the only one of the three that is simultaneously a logo and a working UI component, and the ruled name is the only lockup with a detail (the crossed descender) that a competitor cannot copy without copying the idea.

Recognition, said eight ways

Grammar borrowed deliberately: balanced antithesis and name-period-claim. No adjectives praise the product; the recall does.

01

The house remembers.

The whole direction in three words, and a sentence a restaurateur already says out loud about their own dining room.

02

Name-period-category-claim. The category claim is not "ops platform" but total recall — which is what an ops platform actually is when it works.

Balanced antithesis aimed at the owner's real trade-off: hours in a spreadsheet versus minutes on the floor.

Two words, verb-first. Turns the software into an object you consult rather than a system you operate.

08

The benefit stated as relief, not capability — the sentence a tired manager most wants to hear at 23:00.

§04Voice — how the book speaks

It greets you, then it gets out of the way

Warm because it knows you; never chatty, never cute. Warmth arrives quoted — Marta's reply, Deniz's note — while Mudavym's own sentences stay plain and exact.

  • Name first, always.People and places lead the sentence; numbers and IDs follow. "Vinos del Norte shorted you once — July 2025", never "PO-1042 has a quantity discrepancy".
  • Recall carries its date.Every claim of memory shows its evidence: "since March", "your fourth Thursday running", "last time, $76.20". Recall without a date is a boast.
  • A dash where the memory is empty.If the book has nothing, it says so — "—", "we have not met them before". Never a zero, never an invented comparison.
  • Second person, present tense, no exclamation.The house is addressing one person standing at the pass. It never shouts and never congratulates itself.
  • Serif once per screen — the moment of recognition.The greeting or the verdict is set in Newsreader. Everything else works in sans and counts in mono.
  • Warmth arrives quoted.Vendor replies and staff notes appear in italics, attributed, in their own words. The brand around them stays deadpan.
  • Plain trade words."Bottles in", "the door", "the book", "letters", "the night" — never SKU, sync, workflow, entity, resource.
  • Endings are said in full.Deletion and closure copy states exactly what will be forgotten, in whole sentences, with no euphemism and no dark pattern.

§05Palette — warm paper, cool ink, one gilt material

Signed in violet, because registers were

The accent is signing violet — aniline violet was the standard ink of civil registers and hotel books for a century, and it is nowhere near the blue-indigo-emerald band every startup is standing in. Paper stays warm; the ink ladder is cast slightly violet, so text and paper read the way a written page actually reads. Gilt is a material, not a status: tally marks, ribbons and page edges only — never a fill, never text, never a state.

Rules that keep it disciplined: one accent on screen at a time; every status hue ships with its 10–13% tint twin so state never sits raw on paper; charts draw in the ink ladder with chroma only where meaning is (the anomaly, the shortfall, the line that needs a decision); and gilt never becomes a status — the moment gold means "warning", the recognition system dies. Every ink step is measured, not eyeballed: ink-3 clears AA at 4.6:1 (light) and 5.3:1 (dark) because dates and ordinals are content here, not chrome; ink-4 — the colour of the honest — — is held above 3:1 in both themes for the same reason.

§06Type — the name-first hierarchy

The largest word in any row is somebody's name

This is the thesis, and it is a real inversion: ops software leads with the identifier because the database does. A guest book leads with the name, because a person does. Newsreader carries every name and every moment the house speaks; Instrument Sans does the work; DM Mono holds the dates and the money in tabular columns that never shiver.

§07Motion — the hand that writes, and the page that turns

Ink is laid down; it does not bounce

Two physical models and nothing else. Writing — strokes that draw along their own length, left to right, in the order a hand would make them. Turning — surfaces that open on a long settle, never a snap. One emphatic exception: each return-mark lands with a touch of overshoot, because being counted again should feel like something.

tokens ruleDraw → markSet → noteSurface, orchestrated as recall (≈880ms total).
