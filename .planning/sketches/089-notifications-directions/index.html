Sketch 089 · /notifications · two directions

The two the page note argued against.

Both were considered and rejected in the first pass; the founder asked to see them, full of data, before the fork is closed.

The shipping rebuild is the one-column day-book: three registers stacked, one direction of travel, a line moving down as it is worked. These two files draw the alternatives at full density — every register the gateway actually produces, folded duplicates, digests, the calm band, the ruled-off history, the rail tallies and every filter the gateway already accepts. Neither is a working page; both are screenshots of one.

A · the three-column desk

Everything at once

The three registers side by side under a fixed count strip, each scrolling on its own. Nothing is below the fold. The cost: a worked line teleports across a column boundary instead of travelling down, and three live columns read as a monitoring wall.

B · the day-strip

The book as a diary

An hour axis for today, the day’s lines hung off it in the order written, and the week beside it as a stack of closed accounts under the double rule. The cost: the whole page spent on the one dimension every row already states in five characters.

What is drawn that is not built

Marked in both files with a dashed seal edge and the word proposed: the per-line rule and its history (threshold, observed value, 90-day fire count, “this rule is too loud”) needs a rule-history read the gateway does not have; and subdue-by-settlement — a line greying out when the credit note lands or the PO goes out rather than when someone reads it — needs the settlement events wired to the row. Both are the competitive lens’s “need it: now” ideas for this page and both are filed in the page note §13. Everything else on these pages exists behind a real endpoint today.
