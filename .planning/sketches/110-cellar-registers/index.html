How a register is looked at.

Sketch 092 asked what the parent is (a floor, or one table). Sketch 095 merged them: registers the house carries, a floor strip of confirmed zones, a whole-cellar table. This pass asks the question those two left open. A register is open. What do you actually see?

Direction A

The List

One plain list per register. Producer, name, vintage, the list price. Depth lives in the margin as a hairline of stock against the bottle's own par — not a column competing with the name. Choose a row and the margin is the reading.

the shipped table, with the columns that were crowding it taken off the row

 Direction B

The Gazetteer

The cellar as a place. You walk a confirmed zone and read each bottle as an entry in the house's own words — first bought, what we poured, who counted it, the memo someone here actually wrote. A gazetteer of this building, not a catalogue of the trade.

 Direction C

The Wall

Bottles laid out as a wall of tiles you scan with your eyes the way you scan a cellar wall. Each tile fills from the bottom with what is on hand. Below-par wears the seal ring. Off this read is an empty slot. A caption bar names the one you are looking at.

the Shelf view, taken at its word

What all three are being compared against

The shipped cellar (behind mudavym_design_cellar, sketch 095): a parent of the registers this house carries, In the building tonight, a floor strip of confirmed zones, and a register that is an eight-to-ten-column table with a reading stand above it. Breadth on the page, depth on the stand. Character is Fraunces on the titles and the seal on the hold that sends a real order.

The founder's verdict on the first rebuild was crowded, and that he used to like the page where we could see everything, and to put more character into it. The stand was the answer that made those two sentences compatible. These three directions each pick a different sentence to obey first.

The fork, stated once

A keeps "see everything" as a list you can run a finger down, and puts character in the margin rather than in more columns. It is the shipped register with the crowding actually removed, not moved onto a stand. It needs nothing the gateway does not already serve.

B is the brand thesis as a page: one house's memory, not CellarTracker's 7.5 million strangers. It is also the one that cannot be built honestly today. The house ledger is dark until its migration is applied; 76% of library notes are inferred, not recalled; most bottles have no memo a person here wrote. Drawn with invented sentences it would be a picture of a cellar that remembers more than it does.

C is how a person actually looks at a cellar: not by reading a table, by scanning a wall. It answers "where is the Yakut" in the time it takes the eye to travel. It trades away the founder's "see everything" the moment the house carries more than a couple of hundred titles — a wall of 500 tiles is a mosaic, not a list.

Same invented house on every direction (Kadıköy Meyhane, five registers, four confirmed zones). Soft drinks are not drawn, because this house does not carry them. Two zones are not drawn, because nobody here has confirmed them.
