What the live region would say

“Through the till swapped with The reading. It is now at column 1 of 12, row 1.”

This is the pattern the research argues FOR, and the one that was not built. Atlassian's pragmatic drag and drop accessibility guidance is explicit: do not build directional keyboard drag, give each item a menu of named outcomes instead — “directional arrow movement does not translate well to all experiences”, menus avoid screen-reader mode switching, and they user-tested as more discoverable.

Why it loses here. Their board has nameable destinations: a column, a position in a list. A twelve-column canvas does not — the outcomes ARE the coordinates, and a menu that enumerated them would be twelve columns times n rows, which is not a menu. What this direction can honestly offer is the short list above: swaps and a handful of sizes. That is strictly less than the reader can do with a pointer, which is the definition of a second-class path.

What was taken from the argument instead: the announcement discipline (name the item and both positions), and the insistence that a menu-like set of BUTTONS exists — the placing bar in direction A is that, without pretending a coordinate is a name.
