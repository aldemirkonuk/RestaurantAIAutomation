One slip, one act.

Every folio carries a single slip at its head, and the slip holds exactly one thing to do: the act that unblocks the most, its reason, what comes after it, and the control that does it. When the act lands the slip advances; when the page is ruled off the slip becomes the double rule. The owner never searches the page for what to do next, and the ledger beneath stays fully live — the slip points, it never hides.

Next on this pageSeal ribbon. One act, its reason, and after it what comes next — named, and counted as what is left, never as a percentage.
In pencilGrey ribbon. Mudavym heard or read it; the slip says it waits on the one seal on fo. 5 and offers to turn there.
Ruled offNo ribbon. The slip is the double rule: who, when, and the word to reopen. Reopening strikes the value in place and writes nothing until a new one is posted.
Carried forwardGrey ribbon, no fill. Offered, skipped, dated. The slip offers to answer it now.
RefusedInk ribbon. The register's words verbatim, nothing written, the act offered again. Not dated on the line: nothing records a refusal.
Opens whenGrey ribbon. What unblocks the page, and that act instead.
