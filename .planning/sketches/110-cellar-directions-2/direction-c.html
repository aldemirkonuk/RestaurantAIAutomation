The Wall.

The cellar page is drawn, not listed: every bottle the house keeps is a spine on a shelf, filled to what is on hand against its own par. A register is a shelf; a style is a run of spines on it. "See everything" is literal — 105 wines are 105 marks on one screen, 96 on the shelf and 9 on the off-list plank — and the page is not crowded because a spine carries no text until you point at it. The honesty is in the drawing itself: a spine the cellar cannot count is hollow, so a whisky bar sees at a glance that its main shelf is outlines, and a café sees a wall of outlines and a sentence saying why. A spine opens its record in a side sheet — one object — and the table the founder already has is one switch away, as the Ledger. The h1 keeps the name the founder already decided (The Cellar here, Soft drinks for the café — cellar-format.ts:196-225); the Wall is the name of this view, the other half of the Wall/Ledger switch. Every 1440 frame is drawn with the invoice book and the recipe book populated and says so; the Partial state is the ship state. The seal sits in the record sheet and nowhere else — drawn mid-pour in §5 on Boğazkere, 9 against a par of 12.
