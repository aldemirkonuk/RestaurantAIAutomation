080 · Mudavym brand direction · OD-106 · one of five

The Pass.

Named for the rail where the expo calls the order and the whole kitchen answers. Display type you can read at arm's length, one verb per action, springs that arrive fast and settle faster. High energy without chaos — Mudavym as the calm loud voice that runs service.

01 — Logo

Three marks off the rail.

All three set in Archivo 900. The V is the tick — the approve gesture hiding inside the name.

A — The Rail

Primary lockup. The heavy rule is the pass rail; the two nubs are clipped tickets. The fire V doubles as a checkmark — every approval re-draws the logo.

B — The Chit

App icon + favicon candidate. An order ticket clipped to the rail, serrated where it tears. The M sits on the chit the way an order sits on the line: visible, owned, in progress.

C — The Stamp

The expo's "seen" stamp, tilted like it was slammed by hand. Softest lockup of the three (lowercase, mono strapline) — for invoices, vendor emails, places the brand signs its work.

02 — Slogans

Say it once. Loud.

Six candidates. Structures borrowed from the reference set: verb-first deadpan, balanced antithesis, and "Name. Category claim."

Runs the pass.

Three words that claim the operational center of every restaurant. Deadpan, verb-first — the brand never praises itself.

Call it. Done.

The expo call-and-response compressed to four syllables. Mirrors the product loop exactly: one tap to approve, Mudavym executes.

Less chaos, more service.

Balanced-antithesis structure. Names the enemy (chaos) and the customer promise (service) in six syllables.

The loudest calm in your kitchen.

States the brand paradox outright — expo-line energy with expo-line discipline. The line people repeat back.

Fire when ready.

Kitchen verb doubling as permission language. Fits the approve-gated AI: nothing fires until the operator says fire.

Mudavym. Your restaurant, expedited.

"Name. Category claim." long-form for the website hero. The expo/expedite pun does the brand story in one word.

03 — Voice

One verb per call.

The expo doesn't narrate. Eight rules, then the required rewrites.

R1

Lead with the verb. Then stop.

"Approve." "Count." "Send." A period is a full instruction. Buttons are one word wherever the object is already on screen.

R2

Numbers before nouns.

"7 low. 3 pending." The operator scans quantities first; the noun is confirmation, not headline.

R3

One line per call.

No subordinate clauses on action surfaces. If a sentence needs a comma, it's two calls — split it.

R4

Say the next action, not the system state.

Empty states point at the door, not the void. Never "No data available" — always what to do about it.

R5

Shout once per screen.

One imperative per card, everything else stands down. Two shouts on one surface is chaos, and chaos is the enemy.

R6

Confirm in the past tense.

"Approved." "Sent." "Counted." The kitchen answers the call back — every action echoes its own verb, completed.

R7

Time is short and relative.

"2m ago." "Tonight." "By Fri." Timestamps in full only inside documents and audit trails.

R8

Destruction gets the full sentence.

The one place the voice slows down. Irreversible actions are spelled out in complete, unhurried sentences — no verb-slamming near the edge.

04 — Palette

Stainless, ticket paper, one flame.

05 — Type

128 shouts, 11 whispers.

Two faces only. Archivo (500–900) is the voice — one grotesque stretched from 128px calls down to 13px labels. JetBrains Mono is the machine — every number, code, timestamp and kicker, always tabular. The ~12:1 hero-to-kicker ratio IS the layout.

Archivo — the expo

Google Fonts, variable 100–900. We use 500 body · 700 emphasis · 800 heads · 900 display. Tracking tightens as size grows: −.01em at 18px → −.04em at 128px.

JetBrains Mono — the ticket

Google Fonts. 400–700, tabular-nums always on. Owns kickers (11px, +.14em, uppercase), PO codes, KPI figures, kbd hints, timestamps. Numbers never render in Archivo.

06 — Motion

Arrive hot. Settle instantly.

Interruptible spring physics, never hard-coded easing choreography. Everything lands inside 400ms and nothing oscillates twice — the discipline of a good expo line. Tokens map 1:1 to the motion React library and later to Reanimated withSpring.
