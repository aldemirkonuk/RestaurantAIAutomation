The idea

One delivery at a time. Four numbers per line. A ledger under every line.

The desk is a reconciliation grid: every line of the delivery is a row, and the row carries the four numbers the whole domain is about — ordered · at the door · on paper · the difference in words — then the verdict, which is the current state derived from that line's entries, and the answer the house still owes (ADR 0103 A11: a difference must be answered). The paper column is drawn on paper (ADR 0104 D9's one escape from the charcoal ground) so what the vendor's document says is never confused with what the house counted. Pressing a line turns it open on its own append-only ledger: v1, v2, v3 — who, when, evidence — oldest first, nothing erased. Deliveries are tabs across the top, worst first.

What the staff phone is. The grid with two of its four columns: what was ordered and what the porter is about to count. Paper and money are desk columns; they do not exist on this rendering.

The manager's phone (second) keeps the grid as three numbers — ordered, door, paper — and a verdict in full words stacked as on the desk, the paper cell still on paper, and opens a row's ledger as ADR 0112 F9's bottom sheet: detents peek · half · full, the grabber shown because there is more than one. The answer acts are on the sheet; the question they open stays a centred panel, because a panel is for a question on any screen.

"Not yet" is the absence of a verdict, drawn as an absence. The porter's first count on a line becomes that line's v1; every later change is another entry with the porter's name on it.

The last row is the UNORDERED door: whatever is on the pallet and not on the list is counted too, and becomes a line that pairs with nothing until the desk answers it (D5).

The porter sees their own entries by name, including what the manager appended afterwards — the ledger is one ledger.
