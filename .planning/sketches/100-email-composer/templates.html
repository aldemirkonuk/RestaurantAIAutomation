Mudavym

The template library — house-owned, per purpose.

Seven templates, and not one of them starts as a blank page. A template here is a purpose with its merge fields declared, so the composer knows in advance which figures it will have to stand behind.

House-owned, not person-owned. Front separates individual templates from shared ones and lets a teammate see only the shared ones. Mudavym has no reason for the first kind: a letter to a vendor is written in the house's name, from the house's address, about the house's ledger. Every row below belongs to the restaurant, and every row says who touched it last.

In-house creation

A template starts from something the house noticed

the founder's words: “inhouse email creations … creating data from our insights”

Front got the shape right and named it well: its composer has a New from draft control that lifts the letter you have just written into the template editor so you do not retype it. Mudavym starts one step earlier — not from a draft, and certainly not from a blank editor, but from the observation that made the letter worth writing.

Step one

The house notices

An insight is computed on its own schedule and stored with its rule, its window and its evidence. Nobody asked for it.

“Wednesday came in 38% under its average Wednesday, and the standing order has not changed size in eleven weeks.”

GET /analytics/insights/:rid — exists today

Step two

It offers the letter

The recommendation carries a Write to the vendor door beside the two it already has. Opening it fills the sheet: the vendor from the rule's own entity, the sentence in the body, the chip attached to the sentence.

The manager edits everything except the provenance. You can delete a sentence; you cannot keep its number and drop its chip.

Step three

The letter becomes a template

Save as template keeps the wording and, crucially, keeps the fields rather than the values: the rule key travels, the ₺2.6k does not. Next Wednesday it re-reads.

That is the whole difference between a template in this house and a template anywhere else in the field: ours knows where its numbers come from, so it can refuse to fill one in.

What the field does

Every merge-field system answers a missing value by inventing a plausible one

This was the most useful thing the survey turned up, and it is unanimous. Not one product treats “I could not measure this” as a distinct state.

ProductWhat a merge field doesWhat happens when the value is missing
GmailTemplates are canned bodies; the composer's help pages describe no variables at all. Smart Compose predicts the next words as you type.Google's own caveat: Smart Compose “is not designed to provide answers and may not always predict factually correct information.” Fluency without a source is the default.
HubSpotPersonalization tokens read a contact property.A default value, set globally per property or overridden per email. The reader cannot tell a real value from a default.
MailchimpMerge tags such as *|FNAME|*.Inserts the value “if it's available” — otherwise nothing, silently.
lemlistLiquid, {{variable|fallback}}.The documentation's own advice is to “always use fallbacks” and to test with leads that have empty fields — i.e. to make the substitute invisible.
MissiveLiquid, with a confirm flag.default: "there" substitutes; confirm pauses for a human to look. The closest thing in the field to our chip — but it confirms the insertion, never the measurement.
SuperhumanBuilt-in variables plus free placeholders in curly brackets.Warns you to fill them in before sending. Also a real precedent — and also about text the human owes, not about a figure the system owes.
FrontShared and individual templates; {{ lists dynamic variables; third-party object variables are Enterprise-only.Not stated. The strong idea we take is New from draft, not the variables.
Notion MailAI drafts a whole reply; snippets are saved via the {} control.Not applicable — and worth stating plainly: Notion Mail shuts down on 22 September 2026, so it is a reference, not a model.
Toast xtraCHEFBuilds a purchase order and emails the PDF to the vendor contact, or downloads it.No free-text templating documented. It sends an order, not a letter.
SquarePurchase orders to a vendor email, with an optional note and a Preview Email step.One free-text box on a structured order. The closest the POS field gets to a letter.

The field's universal fallback is absence reported as health, told in a letter.

A default value, a silent blank and a fluent prediction are three ways of saying the same untrue thing: that a number was measured when it was not. The house rule is one line and it is the whole sketch: a figure carries its provenance or it goes in as words. Two products — Missive's confirm and Superhuman's placeholder warning — stop to ask a human, which is nearer than the rest; neither of them can say why a figure is missing, because neither of them computed it.
