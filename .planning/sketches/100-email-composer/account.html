The sending identity — whose name the letter leaves in.

A vendor decides in half a second whether a letter is from a restaurant they buy from or from a piece of software. The address is that decision. It is therefore a property of the house, and it is one of the two things a house pays us for.

The founder, 2026-09-03: a house sends either from its own connected mailbox or from a Mudavym subdomain address we provision. Never from a mailbox shared with every other house on the deployment.

That decision closes the largest hole in this wave. Today every letter this product sends leaves from one address for the entire deployment — GMAIL_SENDER_EMAIL, falling back to the literal string notifications@wineops.ai (gmail.service.ts:78-80), then overwritten at connect time with whatever address the deployment's own refresh token belongs to (:126-129). The restaurant cannot see it, cannot change it, and is not named in it. Both options below replace it; the sketch draws them as the two states of one row.

A house that already has @lokantamudavim.com on Google Workspace connects it once. Letters leave from an address the vendor has seen before, SPF and DKIM already pass, and replies land in a mailbox the house owns and can read without us.

Option B · the paid Mudavym address

We provision one on our sending domain

A house with no domain of its own — most of them — takes an address on mail.mudavym.com. We own the domain, the authentication and the deliverability; the house owns the name in front of the @ and the replies come back into the conversation book.

The obligation that comes with option B

Sending for many houses on one domain makes us the sender

The moment letters leave on our domain, Google's sender requirements are ours to meet, not the restaurant's. They are not onerous, but they are not optional and they change the shape of the build.

  • AuthenticationSPF and DKIM on the sending domain, DMARC published, valid forward and reverse DNS, TLS in transit, and DMARC alignment — the From: domain must align with the SPF or DKIM domain. All of that is ours to hold, once, for every house.
  • Complaint rateGoogle's threshold is a spam rate below 0.30% in Postmaster Tools. Our letters go to vendors, not consumers, so the risk is not volume — it is one house writing something a vendor marks as spam and every other house on the subdomain paying for it. This is the argument for the seal on Send, not merely a nice ceremony.
  • UnsubscribeBulk marketing mail needs one-click unsubscribe (List-Unsubscribe-Post). Vendor correspondence is transactional and does not, but the staff broadcast template is on the edge of it. That distinction has to be enforced in code, per template, not left to whoever writes the letter.
  • Per-house separationOne house's reputation must not sink another's. The measurable version: sub-users or per-house DKIM selectors on the one domain, so a bad sender can be isolated instead of the domain being burned. Not decided, and it is an engineering decision, not a page.

Register III

Where this row lives

Not here. This sketch draws the sending identity in detail because the composer cannot be honest without it — but the row belongs on Connections, the house-scoped surface decided in DESIGN-FOUNDATION.md §6b and drawn in sketch 097, which the founder has today settled as its own route, role-gated.

  • On ConnectionsThe sending identity sits in Register I beside the till, the payment provider, the calendar feed and the public page — the things that act in the house's name. §6b already lists it there and marks it “should be the house's; is nobody's”. This sketch is what fixes that line.
  • On /profileNothing. A person's own Google sign-in link stays on their profile; the mailbox the house writes from was never personal, even when a person's grant is what authorises it. profile.md §13a makes the same cut.
  • On /communicationsOne line, read-only, at the top of the composer: the address this letter will leave from, or the sentence saying there is none. It links to Connections; it does not duplicate the controls.
  • Who may change itA manager or an owner. Every member may see it, because everyone who writes a letter needs to know whose name it leaves in — and because a staff member discovering after the fact that their letter went out as notifications@wineops.ai is precisely the surprise this row exists to prevent.
