The idea. One six-column grid where a tier is a span, not a row: the hero takes 3×3 cells, a large card 2×2, a compact card 1×1, and the grid packs densely so the small ones fill the gaps beside the big ones — the closest to direction B's "bigger sale, bigger box" with C's ten-plus visible. A bundle is tied cells: one small cell per bottle, joined by a bracket that carries the vendor, the name and the bundle's total — the bundle reads as several offers that only come together. Order is rank order; dense packing can move a small card up beside a big one, so reading order and visual order can differ.

What this direction asks you

  • Dense packing lets a compact card sit above a large one it outranks — acceptable, or must visual order equal rank order (costs empty cells)?
  • Hero at 3×3 of a 6-column grid (half the width) — or full width, which pushes everything else below the fold?
  • Tied cells: does a bundle read as one offer, or as several? A bundle of eight bottles would need two rows of ties.
  • Where does the bundle sit — at the rank its total earns (here, among the large), or always after the single offers?
