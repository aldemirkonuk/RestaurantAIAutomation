Two other ways to rule the book

The shipped rebuild files entries by consequence — Money · Stock · Vendors · The floor · Unfiled. These are the two directions its page note has named as considered-and-not-built since the first pass, drawn out in full so the fork can actually be judged rather than described. Both keep the three facts (would change · whose hand · standing) and both carry the new dismissal dialogue, so the choice is about the SHAPE of the page, not about what it can do.

090a

The run-sheet

Banded by time-to-act — Tonight · This week · This month — with the register demoted to a filter strip across the top and a margin column for the leaves, the excluded days and the keys. One sheet, printable, carried to the pass.

Costs: it re-centres the page on urgency, which is the axis the legacy feed already had — so it answers "more structure" and not "more uniqueness". Gains: on a Friday it is the more operational page, and "what must happen before service" is a question the register cannot answer.

 090b

The two-pane docket

The archetype in the page note's own frontmatter (list+detail): the register left, a narrow list of entries in the middle, and the full working — rationale, rule key, the dismissal sheet, the seal — pinned open on the right. Nothing collapses; the working is always visible for the selected entry.

Costs: it reads beautifully with a long book and is dead space with a short one — and short is what a restaurant without POS coverage actually gets. Gains: the working stops being a disclosure and becomes the page, which is the strongest possible answer to "no model wrote this sentence".

Both are one refactor away from the shipped build: the register, the three-fact line, the denominator, the dismissal scopes and the day exclusion survive either. The dismissal dialogue is drawn open in each so its weight can be judged in context — it is the heaviest new thing on the page, and where it sits (inline under the entry, or in a pinned pane) is a real part of the choice.
