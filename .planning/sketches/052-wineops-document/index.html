The WineOps document, rendered three ways by role

One delivery. One MatchResult. Three surfaces, chosen deterministically by who is looking — not learned, not personalised, not adaptive.

This delivers what the agent-native pitch promised — “each user gets a different experience” — with none of the machinery that cannot be validated at n=1 restaurant. Same role, same layout, always. No telemetry, no experiment, no holdout, and it is unit-testable.

Why these three, and what each deliberately refuses

  • The physical count is never pre-filled. Not from the invoice, not from the packing slip, not from the PO. Pre-filling it makes the billed-vs-counted check compare a number to itself and pass trivially — the exact bug this product exists to fix. The receiver types what they see or taps “all here”.
  • Staff see no prices, and that is a feature. It removes the largest source of hesitation at the door, and line cost is not floor-staff information. It also means a “something's off” tap is about bottles, never about money.
  • Matched is not a task. The manager queue shows only discrepancies. The count of hidden matched lines is stated so the omission is visible rather than mysterious — including, explicitly, the split case and the agreed 11-for-10, the two false alarms most likely to destroy trust in the queue.
  • The owner card leads with drift, not dollars recovered. Recoverable billing error on a $1.2M bistro is roughly $150–800/month — real, but it does not carry the story. Silent price creep across hundreds of SKUs is structurally larger, verifiable monthly, and does not depend on a vendor agreeing to anything.
  • Absence is rendered, never blank. “No invoice on file” is a visible state with its own action. A blank is what allowed price_verified: true to be written for deliveries nobody ever looked at.
  • Ship the staff view as mobile web first. Receiving happens at a door, not a desk.
