MUDAVYM Direction 086 — Warm Machine
00 — Premise

The mechanism is the hospitality.

An espresso machine hides nothing. Brass, steel, a pressure gauge on the front, the group head where you can watch the shot pull — and the whole apparatus exists to put a warm cup in someone's hand. Mudavym is built the same way: you can see the agent read, reason and draft, in the open, in numbers — and it is warm to look at while it does it.

The thesis: the most technical direction can also be the warmest. Warmth comes from material and light — brass hairlines, machined corners, a lamp over the working surface. Precision comes from the information design, which never softens.

01

Logo

Three resolved marks. All three are mechanism drawn as letterform — a manifold, a gauge, a group head — with round joins and one brass fitting each. Every mark reads at 16px, holds as a one-colour stamp, and has a defined live state where the machine is working. Brass is the metal; ember is the heat.

A — The Manifold

The M brazed out of pipe

The letter is built the way a boiler manifold is built: two risers, elbowed over, joined at a centre fitting. Round joins and 6px stroke keep it warm at any size; the brass union at the junction is the only chroma. Live state: the centre fitting fills ember while an agent is running — the mark itself is a status lamp. This is the primary lockup.

B — The Gauge

The M is the needle, at working pressure

An M sits inside a brass bezel with its valley exactly on the pivot — so the letter's right arm is the needle, and it points into the patina band at 9.1 bar. The bezel carries the two bands every espresso gauge carries: patina = working, scald = over-pressure. The mark is therefore never neutral: it is always showing the machine in the green. Favicon and app-icon strength — the only mark that holds as a 16px square with the bands still legible.

C — The Group Head

Three streams into one cup

A brass group bar, three streams of unequal length — the machine's three outputs: the order, the count, the reply — and the cup that catches all of them. The whole argument of the direction in one picture: visible apparatus at the top, something warm handed over at the bottom. The ember crema line is the only chroma in the cup. This is the marketing and packaging lockup; A stays the product mark.

Wordmark — two registers

Product register: Bricolage Grotesque 600 at −0.03em — a grotesque with hand in it, so the name never reads machine-generated. Stamp register: IBM Plex Mono 500 in brass, +0.57em tracked caps — for the machine's own output: printed POs, exported receipts, the top bar. The brass colour is reserved to the stamp register; the product register is always ink.

03

Voice

Unlike a deadpan instrument brand, this product is allowed to be warm in the verb — but it buys that warmth with a number in every sentence. It never chirps, never apologises, and never states a conclusion without the reading it came from.

V-01

Show the working, then the answer.

No recommendation ships without the reading beneath it. "3.3 nights of cover — 7 bottles, 2.1 a night." A conclusion with no visible arithmetic is a black box, and this brand's entire claim is that it isn't one.

V-02

Warmth in the verb, precision in the number.

The sentence may be kind; the figure may never be approximate. "About a dozen" is banned; "12 bottles, $930" is the same sentence done properly. Warmth is never bought by being vague.

V-03

The machine says "I". Facts say nothing.

First person belongs only to agent actions — I read, I drafted, I'm holding this. Measurements, totals and states are stated flat, with no speaker. The reader always knows who is talking.

V-04

Physical verbs from a real kitchen.

Draft, pour, hold, seat, count, receive, approve, hand off, set down, close. Never manage, leverage, optimise, streamline, empower. If two hands can't do it, the verb is wrong.

V-05

No exclamation, no apology, no confetti.

A finished job reports its number, not its feelings. "Sent. 12 bottles, $930." Never "Success!", never "Oops — something went wrong". Celebration is the operator's, not the software's.

V-06

Empty is a reading, not an absence.

Every empty state names the measurement that is missing and the condition that fills it. "No data" is a shrug; "6 nights counted, 14 needed" is an instrument reporting honestly.

V-07

Consequences in objects, not warnings.

Destructive copy lists what physically disappears — orders, counts, threads, years. Never "This action cannot be undone" alone; name the inventory being destroyed and let the number do the warning.

V-08

One sentence, one thing.

A second thought is a second line. Fragments are legal and often better: "Held for you." is a complete message. Nothing in this product needs a paragraph.

04

Palette

No near-black, no neon. Dark mode is roast — a warm coffee-black with brown in it — and light mode is crema, not white. The accents are drawn straight off the machine: brass is the metal (structure, never status), ember is heat (working, or waiting on a human), patina is oxidised copper (settled, healthy), scald is over-pressure (irreversible money). No indigo, no violet, no startup blue anywhere in the system.

The four laws. ① Brass is a material, not a state — it draws bezels, stamp type, section hairlines and the fill on primary buttons, and it never means "something happened". ② Ember means a human is required — it marks what the machine is working on or waiting on, never a status and never a brand moment. Tiles may count what is waiting; only one card per screen may ask. ③ Patina is the resting state, so a healthy screen is warm-grey and green, never blank. ④ Scald is reserved for money that cannot come back — over-pressure, nothing less. Charts draw in the warm-grey ramp; hue enters a chart only where meaning does.

06

Motion

Brass has mass. Everything in this system moves like a machined part being seated: it accelerates, it settles, and the heavier the object the longer it takes. Three of the eight tokens are real springs (stiffness / damping / mass), sampled into CSS linear() here and shipped as true spring configs to motion and Reanimated. Nothing bounces twice.
