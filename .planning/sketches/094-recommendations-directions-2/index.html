Three ways to make the book tell you what to do

The founder, on the fourth pass: “I also liked the day strip a lot … a calendar strip that we can select and see that is highly advanced and elegant looking, or sth else. The need is that we need to everything in a categorized classified section in order for people to understand what to do as action.” Two wants and one need. These three files answer the need three ways, and each draws the two new buttons and the dismissal sheet so the fork is about the shape of the page, not about what it can do.

094a

The calendar strip

Twenty-eight days across the top, one cell per day: what fired, what falls due, what it is worth, and — the part nobody else draws — which days the house has no records for, hatched rather than zeroed. Select a day and the book below becomes that day; hover an entry and a hairline draws its life back across the strip to the day it was first shown.

Costs: a recommendation has no natural date. Most entries share one first-fired day, so on a real tenant the strip can be a single loaded column beside twenty-seven empty ones. Gains: it gives the day-exclusion control a home (double-click a day to strike it), and it is the only direction that makes “this rule has stood for 17 days” visible rather than stated.

 094b

The action docket

Filed by the act your hands perform: order it · price it · move stock · call a vendor · brief the floor — each with its count, its money at stake, and one line saying what doing the whole section looks like. The founder’s fifth heading, change a rule, is drawn dark because nothing behind it exists.

Costs: nine entries over five headings is often one row per heading, and headings over singletons read as ceremony. Gains: it is the literal answer to “understand what to do as action”, it needs one nine-row mapping and nothing else, and it turns nine decisions into five sittings.

 094c

The case ledger

The unit stops being a card and becomes a case: opened → acted → watching → closed with a result, plus refused as a state with its reason kept. A closed case carries what actually happened — or “no comparable data”, which is a different answer from zero. Make this a goal is the instrument that moves a case into watching.

Costs: the outcome column needs a measurement job that does not exist, and a case store the disposition table is only four-fifths of. Months, not days. Gains: it is the only direction that would make this page unlike every competitor’s; DESIGN-FOUNDATION §6 already names it the need-it-now idea for this surface.
The recommendation

Build 094b as the spine. Put 094a above it as a ribbon, not as the axis. Keep 094c as the roadmap.

The founder named the calendar strip as a want and the classified sections as the need, and the evidence agrees with his own ordering. A recommendation has no date of its own: the rules are re-evaluated against tonight’s numbers on every request, and the only date most entries carry is firstSeenAt — which is capped at 40 keys and null wherever no impression was recorded. A page whose primary axis is time would, on the tenant we can actually measure, put every entry it has in one column and leave twenty-seven blank. The act, by contrast, is knowable for every entry from a field the feed already returns.

So: sections by the act, with count and money at stake, are the spine — that is 094b, and it costs one nine-row mapping. The strip goes above it and does the two things a strip is genuinely good at: showing when (first fired, falls due, and which days have no records at all) and being the home for the day-exclusion control, which today is a list in a side panel and should be a day you strike. Nothing is selected by default, so the strip is information until it is asked to be a filter.

The strongest counter-argument, and why it loses. “Two objects on one page is two organising principles, and a page with two spines has none — you have just rebuilt the monitoring wall that the notifications three-column direction was rejected for, and you have added a third cut (act) on top of the register (consequence) that the page already has.” That is the right worry and it fails on one distinction: the ribbon is not an axis, it is a selector, and when nothing is selected the book below is the whole book. The register is not a competing table of contents either — under this proposal it stops being the page’s spine and becomes the ordering within a section and the filter across them, so the reader still meets exactly one classification at the top level. What survives of the objection, honestly, is the thin-book case: on a house with three standing entries, five headings over five singletons is ceremony. The answer is that a heading over one entry still carries the count and the money, which a bare card does not — but it is the real cost of this choice and it should be judged on the gallery, not on this page.

What the field does, and what to refuse

Google Ads Recommendations groups by the object that changes — bidding, budgets, keywords, ads, assets — with apply-all and dismiss-all per type. It is the closest thing to a docket anyone ships, and its optimization score reaches 100% by dismissing everything: a queue-clearing metric wearing the clothes of health. Group like it; never score like it.
AWS Trusted Advisor gives suppression a home — excluded per resource, scoped, and checks inherited from Security Hub say they are not yours to silence.
Google Search Console is the lifecycle: an issue is grouped by type, you fix it, you request validation, and it passes or fails per URL, then stays closed for 90 days before the same fault counts as new. 094c is that machine.
Stripe Radar’s review queue is the same object at transaction scale: a prioritised list, risk insights that say why the score is what it is, J/K between items, self-assignment, and review.closed carrying a reason.
Linear Triage gives an unclassified queue exactly four resolutions — accept, duplicate, decline, snooze — and Linear’s cycles decide what happens to what was not finished.
Things 3 classifies by commitment, not by date: Today, Anytime, Someday — a task with no date is not overdue, it is Anytime. Todoist’s Upcoming is the closest working calendar strip: a week navigator across the top, days selected below. Asana’s My Tasks moves work between time sections by rule, and stopped auto-promoting because moving someone’s work without being asked was worse than leaving it. Superhuman’s Split Inbox is the strongest argument for 094b: dividing one stream into named rooms is what makes triage fast, and the rooms are chosen by the reader’s job, not by the sender.
Datadog’s Watchdog insight cards carry their evidence — the proportion of errors a tag contributes against baseline — which is the standard our working pane already meets.
Toast IQ executes menu and shift edits from the feed card. That is the one to refuse: an act that cannot be reconstructed later.

The two new buttons, built and drawn

Make this a goal writes a real goal: POST /analytics/goals/:rid, with the metric, the direction and the period derived from the rule that fired, the name derived and editable, and only the target asked of the manager — a rule states a gap, not a number a house should be held to. Nine of the thirteen rules map to one of the gateway’s six metrics; three map to none (a stockout is availability, an HHI is not a figure a goal can be held on) and render dark with the reason; the goal-behind family refuses because it is already a goal.

See it in reports sends each rule to the one cutting of the sheet’s eleven whose register answers it — and says whether that is the same register the rule read or a different one that plots the same thing. Four rules have no cutting at all and say so rather than sending you to a drawing that is not about them.

Both are live behind mudavym_design_recommendations today. Everything drawn with a dashed proposed mark in these three files is not.
