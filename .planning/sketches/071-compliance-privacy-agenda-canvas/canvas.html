The gate is two gates — and the blocking half is the one nobody built

The department has treated "the consent gate" as one thing. Media & Brand gates on two subjects, and the good schema solves only one of them.

Guests

ASSERTED — schema exists

Consent as a versioned record: consent_purpose, consent_notice_version, consent_captured_via (CHECK, 4 channels), consent_withdrawn_at. Erasure as a tombstone. 564 lines, argued in-file, CI-guarded — and zero call sites.

vs

Customers (restaurants)

ABSENT — no record at all

Public web presence may only be reviewed with the restaurant's explicit approval. The approval register does not exist, so today the correct answer to every research request is no. This is the half that blocks them.

Why this is good news for a schedule: the blocking half is B2B. No ML cost, no append-only tension, no NF-B dependency — a record, a purpose vocabulary, a withdrawal path. It is small, and nobody had said so in one sentence before this canvas.

The derivation trap — this repo's only key precedent cannot shred

ADR 0037 locked crypto-shredding: per-guest keys, erasure = key destruction. An implementer will reach for the one key-management pattern already in the tree. It is the wrong one, and the reason is not obvious.

What exists — guest_pepper()

vault: guest_identifier_pepper │ (one master secret) ▼ HMAC(restaurant_id, master) key(restaurant A) key(restaurant B) │ │ ▼ ▼ hashes hashes
Derived. You cannot forget one output of a deterministic function — destroying the master shreds every subject under it. Fine for a pepper. Fatal for erasure.

What ADR 0037 needs

KEK │ ▼ wraps key(guest 1) key(guest 2) key(guest 3) │ ✂ destroyed │ ▼ ▼ rows rows = noise rows
Stored, not derived — one destroyable key per subject. Structurally the opposite of the precedent, which is exactly why it has to be written down before anyone builds it.
The question underneath (D2): destroying a key in the live database does not destroy its copy in a PITR window. Crypto-shredding's real advantage is that the backup becomes noise too — but only if the key was never in the backup in the same restorable form. That is a placement decision, and it is the same drill SRE already owes for days_since_verified_restore.

Found while writing this agenda

A fourth PII definition, shipping today

AuthContext.tsx:208-218 sends the signed-in user's email, name and restaurant id to Sentry on every authenticated session. beforeSend strips exactly two things — the authorization and cookie headers. That is an unwritten definition: auth headers are sensitive; an email address is not — and it disagrees with research_tasks.py, for which an email is PII, inside the same product.

A public promise with a hole in it

Privacy.tsx enumerates five flows — cookies, Google sign-in, integrations, product analytics, partner sharing. Error tracking is not among them. The analytics section's careful list of what leaves the browser is scoped to interaction telemetry, while a second exporter ships an email address.

Four days after they were written: constraint_engine.py :28→:52, applied :113→:137. provider_communication_agent.py applied :725→:833. research_tasks.py has two call sites, not one. One of four held. A department whose entire product is citations cannot keep them by hand.
