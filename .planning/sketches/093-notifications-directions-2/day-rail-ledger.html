Open the price ladder

The rule, in full: the newest sighting of a product against the mean of its 3 or more earlier sightings in the last 30 days, per 750ml equivalent, this house’s own quotes plus public list prices. The latest is not folded into its own average. This box reads prices; it never places an order.

Measured 2026-09-03: vendor_price_observations holds zero rows on the project this gateway reads, so on the real product this box today says “the price register holds no sightings at all” — not “nothing is cheap”. The figures above are the sketch’s invention.

What this direction costs

The book stays one column, so a line still travels down as it is worked and the page still quiets rather than becoming a different page — that is the whole Editorial idea the verdict kept. What the rail buys is the thing the day-strip was actually right about: this register is written one page per day by its own producer, so a day is a real unit here, and picking one is a server-side read rather than a browser trick. What it costs is that the four registers the founder named on 3 September — a goal reached, deliveries, invoice confirmations, sale records — are all records, and they land in the same column as the eight lines that need a hand. On the example data above that is 8 against 18; when deliveries and sales are actually being written it will be 8 against several hundred a week, and “Needs a hand” becomes a short paragraph at the top of a very long page. The register filter and the hide-read fold hold that back; a split would remove it. That is the fork, and it is the reason direction B exists.
