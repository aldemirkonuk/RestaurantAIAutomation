Register, status and the day are the register’s own filters (type · status · dateFrom · dateTo, applied as eq/eq/gte/lte in notifications.service.ts:811-821), so the count they produce is the register’s. The search box and the hide-read fold work on the lines already on this screen; there is no full-text search behind this table.

The idea, in one sentence. A notification is not an event, it is the latest sentence in an account the house is keeping about a thing — this wine, this invoice, this vendor, this goal. Fold by the thing, and the page stops being a list of repetitions and becomes a set of open accounts, each with a state, a rule, a loudness and a way to close. Sentry does exactly this to errors: 718 events collapse into one issue with a fingerprint, a count and a first-seen. Opsgenie does it to alerts: “there can be at most one open alert with the same alias at any time”, and the duplicates raise a counter instead of a new row. Nobody does it to a restaurant’s books, because only a ledger can say when an account settles.

Measured, and this is why it is proposed and not built

An account needs a subject key on every row. The notifications table has three columns that would carry one — related_entity_type, related_entity_id and notification_group — and on 3 September all three are NULL on 663 of 663 rows. The only populated key is group_key, on 662 of 663, and it is not a subject: its 237 distinct values are low_stock_digest:2026-08-16, …:2026-08-17 and so on — a rule and a date, one per day. So every account above would have to be inferred from a title, which is a house guessing at its own records. The fix is one line in the producers, not a page: write related_entity_type and related_entity_id when the row is created. Filed in the page note §13 with the call sites.

Open the price ladder

The rule, in full: the newest sighting of a product against the mean of its 3 or more earlier sightings in the last 30 days, per 750ml equivalent, this house’s own quotes plus public list prices. The latest is not folded into its own average. This box reads prices; it never places an order.

What this direction costs

It is the only one of the three that attacks what the register actually contains rather than how it is arranged. Measured on production: 663 rows, 57 distinct titles, and the first page of a hundred rows collapses to three lines once the existing stacker has folded it — so the layout argument is currently being had about three rows, while the same sentence has been written 147 times. Accounts make that visible instead of hiding it, and they make “subdue by settlement” natural: an account greys out when the credit note lands, not when someone reads it. The cost is that it needs the producers to say what each row is about, and until they do, every subject here is a guess. It is also the furthest from a familiar inbox: an operator who opens it looking for “the new thing” finds a state of affairs instead, and there is no evidence yet that a restaurant manager wants that at 4pm on a Friday.
