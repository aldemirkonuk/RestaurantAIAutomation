Open the price ladder

The rule, in full: the newest sighting of a product against the mean of its 3 or more earlier sightings in the last 30 days, per 750ml equivalent, this house’s own quotes plus public list prices. The latest is not folded into its own average. This box reads prices; it never places an order.

Measured 2026-09-03: vendor_price_observations holds zero rows on the project this gateway reads, so on the real product this box today says “the price register holds no sightings at all” — not “nothing is cheap”. The figures above are the sketch’s invention.

What this direction costs

It is the right answer to the volume problem the founder’s own new registers create: a goal reached, a delivery, an invoice confirmation and a service record are all reports, and once they are being written they outnumber the decisions by two orders of magnitude. A split keeps the left room permanently short, which is the only thing that makes a room worth opening. The costs are three and they are real. One: the split needs a classifier, and today the honest one is a printed list of types — which means a new producer that nobody adds to the list lands silently in the wrong room. Two: measured on the production register on 3 September, 641 of 663 rows are inventory_low_stock and 631 of 663 are critical, so on today’s data everything is a decision and the right-hand room is nearly empty — the split pays off only after the four new registers exist. Three: it breaks the one structural idea the verdict kept, that a worked line travels down the page rather than teleporting; here it jumps a column boundary, which is precisely why the three-column desk was rejected in the first pass.
