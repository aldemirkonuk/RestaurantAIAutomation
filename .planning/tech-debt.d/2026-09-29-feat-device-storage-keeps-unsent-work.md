## Device storage keeps unsent work: what the storage ruling left for later — OPEN — 2026-09-29

Filed by `feat/device-storage-keeps-unsent-work` (the founder's storage ruling, 2026-09-29, verbatim: "option 1, not sent. but we have to find a way to optimizew storage using per device? what sindustry equilavent"). What the branch built: `apps/web/src/lib/deviceStorage.ts` asks `navigator.storage.persist()` once per page load after sign-in or session restore (not on Firefox, where it prompts; there the banner offers a button), `offline-storage.ts` sweeps expired read-cache rows at boot and empties the read cache at sign-out (cache store and its `entity_cache_*` localStorage copies only, never the pending queue), and `AppOfflineBanner` nudges after `UNSENT_NUDGE_AFTER_MS` (24 h) and shows a non-dismissable Add to Home Screen line while unsent work sits on storage that is not persistent. CLAIMS `STORAGE-RULING-2026-09-29-DEVICE-KEEPS-UNSENT-WORK`. The record of the ruling itself goes into ADR 0241 as an amendment after OD-203's parking PR lands (not edited here, to avoid a conflict with that PR).

**Why no byte or count budget.** A queued door receipt is about 1 KB and nothing queues photos or blobs; 100 unsent changes are ~100 KB against a quota that is a large share of the disk. Browsers evict a whole origin, never part of one, so trimming our own queue frees nothing that saves the rest. Workbox Background Sync's 7-day `maxRetentionTime` (delete a queued request for waiting) was rejected: ADR 0241 never drops a change for waiting.

**Deferred, each its own follow-up:**

1. **Server-side dead-letter copy of refused items.** The founder chose "keep a copy on the server" (2026-09-29) over an export file. A change the server refused for good (`parked.reason = 'refused'`) should be written to a server-side dead-letter record so it survives the device and the sign-out, and a manager can see it. Not built: it needs a gateway endpoint and a table (a migration). No device export of parked items is to be built.
2. **An OPFS / blob store.** Only once offline photos exist (a receipt photo, a label scan). Until then IndexedDB rows of ~1 KB are the whole footprint and a blob store would guard nothing.
3. **The legacy `sync-inventory` handler in `apps/web/public/sw.js`** (the `event.tag === "sync-inventory"` branch). Nothing registers that tag from the app's queue; it is a second, unowned sync path in the service worker. Remove or wire it; not touched here.
4. **The swallowed-quota localStorage fallback** is already filed: `v3.0-TECH-DEBT.md:4863`, "The offline queue reports a write it swallowed as a write that succeeded — OPEN — 2026-09-12". Not duplicated here; `persist()` lowers the odds of eviction, it does not make a refused write visible.
5. **An expired session that never reaches `endSession` keeps the read cache.** `lib/sessionRefresh.ts` (a refused refresh) and `services/api/client.ts` (the 401 redirect to `/login`) remove the tokens without calling `AuthContext.endSession`, so the cache is not emptied there. The cache rows expire in 2–5 minutes and the boot sweep removes them after the redirect's reload once expired, but a next person signing in inside that window could read the previous session's unexpired rows. Fix: clear the cache on those paths too, or key cache rows by person and house.
6. **Home Screen on dock devices: decided as a strong hint, not a requirement** (the founder, 2026-09-29). The banner escalates instead: the Home Screen line cannot be dismissed while storage is not persistent and this session has unsent work. Whether a receiving dock tablet should be installed as a matter of setup (an onboarding checklist item) is not built.
7. **`UNSENT_NUDGE_AFTER_MS` = 24 h is decided** (the founder confirmed it, 2026-09-29, after Square's offline mode: upload within 24 h). Listed so a change to it is a decision, not a tweak.

**Not verified.** No real browser was driven: persist() behaviour per engine is taken from MDN ("Storage quotas and eviction criteria") and web.dev ("Persistent storage"), not measured on a device. The Firefox test is a user-agent test (`Firefox/`); a browser that prompts without saying `Firefox/` would be asked unprompted at sign-in.

**Severity:** low while open. Nothing here loses a change today; items 1 and 5 are the ones with a person on the other end.
